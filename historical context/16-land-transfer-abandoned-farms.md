# Land, Abandoned Farms & "Departure" Paperwork (1940s–1950s)

How a house and its land actually changed hands in the depopulated Bieszczady — and why a *notarial sale deed* is the wrong instrument for the 1954 Barnaś cover-up.

## Key facts

- **Depopulated land passed to the State by decree, not by sale.** Property left behind by deported or vanished populations was classed as **mienie opuszczone / poniemieckie i porzucone** (abandoned property). The **decree of 8 March 1946** and related PRL legislation vested such property in the **State Treasury** administratively. There was no willing seller, no notary, no purchase price.
- **Settlers held land by administrative grant, not by title.** Incoming Polish settlers ("repatrianci" from the east, and internal migrants) received farms through an **akt nadania** — an administrative grant issued by the *starosta* or *gminna* authority. Many holdings were never even entered in the land-and-mortgage register (**księga wieczysta**). Ownership was thin, provisional, and bureaucratic.
- **Vacant farms were absorbed by PGR administratively.** When a settler farm fell empty, the State Farm (**PGR**) simply took it onto its books through internal/administrative records. No conveyance between private parties took place — the land was already the State's.
- **A private "sale of the house to the PGR" is therefore historically wrong.** A resident couldn't sell State land they merely held by grant, and the PGR didn't buy what the State already owned.

## What the paperwork realistically was

- The credible document is an **administrative departure / handover declaration** — a form recording that the holders had *left* and their grant lapsed, returning the farm to State/PGR administration. This is filed **through a gmina/PGR office weeks after the fact**, not signed on the night.
- This resolves the "where did they get a deed in the heat of the night?" problem: the night was a **verbal bargain**; any paper came **later**, produced and filed administratively.
- It also sharpens the forgery's purpose: forging the departing holder's signature makes a **disappearance read as a voluntary move west** — routine, unremarkable, one lapsed grant among thousands in a region built on churn.

## Campaign application

- The 1954 Barnaś document is an **administrative departure declaration** handing the farm back to the PGR, **forged weeks after the lynch** — not a notarial sale deed.
- **Marta had nothing to sign away** (the land was the State's, held by grant), so her bargain is her **silence and disappearance**, not a property transfer.
- The **forgery is [Emil Rzepka](../characters/painter.md)'s** hand, at [Helena Rzepka](../characters/matrona.md)'s direction — the architect laundering the vanishing into an official record through the one skill her broken husband has left.
