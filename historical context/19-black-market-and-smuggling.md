# Black Market, Bimber & Smuggling — PRL and the Bieszczady Border Zone

Reference for any character built around illicit trade (the black-market peddler, the bimber economy, contraband). Grounds how a trader-in-secrets could plausibly exist in a poor, remote, tightly-watched 1960s valley.

## The Second Economy Was Universal

Chronic shortage was the baseline of PRL daily life: meat, sugar, coffee, alcohol, and manufactured goods were scarce in state distribution. A parallel, semi-tolerated "black market" (**czarny rynek**) filled the gap everywhere, and rural people leaned on it hard — goods sold *spod lady* ("from under the counter"), barter, and personal *znajomości* (connections) were how families actually got by. The state largely looked the other way rather than deepen the supply crisis.

- Standard reference: **Jerzy Kochanowski, *Tylnymi drzwiami. „Czarny rynek" w Polsce 1944–1989*.**

## The Traveling Trader (wędrowny handlarz)

A characteristic figure: a trader who worked village to village, offering goods missing from state shops and **buying up rationed articles** (meat, dairy, alcohol) to resell. Illegal, but socially accepted — he met real local need and turned a profit doing it. Related stock types: the *baba z cielęciną* (woman selling meat illegally), the *cinkciarz* (currency/ticket dealer, more urban).

**Why he fits the campaign:** in a valley with one poorly-stocked store, an itinerant trader is the natural node of both goods *and* gossip. He sleeps in barns, crosses everyone's threshold, and hears everything — a believable keeper of the village's dirt.

## Bimber (Moonshine)

Home distilling was widespread in the countryside, easy to make from potatoes, grain, or sugar, and traded both locally and **through traveling handlarze**. Already canon here via the [bimber still](../locations/bimber-still.md) and [bimber operation](../story-facts/bimber-operation.md) — a peddler plugging into that trade needs no new justification.

## The Border Zone — the Real Constraint

The Bieszczady in the 1960s were a **strefa nadgraniczna** (border zone): entry and residence restricted, special passes required, patrolled by **WOP** (Wojska Ochrony Pogranicza) and milicja, penetrated by the SB, thick with distrust and informing (*donosicielstwo*).

- **The USSR border was near-sealed** — no-man's-land strips, wide cleared cuts, barbed wire, close monitoring. Smuggling *across the Soviet line* was rare and near-suicidal.
- **The Czechoslovak border was guarded but more porous.** Cross-border smuggling of alcohol, cigarettes, textiles, electronics, even animals ran for decades despite the patrols.
- Smuggling depended on **intimate terrain knowledge** — the wild, road-poor, depopulated mountains, and especially **abandoned villages and forest paths**, were the natural routes. Locals (sometimes even bribed WOP soldiers) helped.

## Plausibility Verdict For the Campaign

- ✅ **Fully grounded:** an internal black-market peddler + bimber trader + fence, working remote villages, holding leverage over people who owe him or buy from him.
- ✅ **Grounded:** occasional contraband runs toward the **Czechoslovak** side.
- ⚠️ **Bend, don't lean on:** a career *cross-USSR-border* smuggler — the Soviet line was too sealed for that to be routine. Keep his smuggling internal / Czechoslovak-leaning.
- 🎯 **Free integration:** his routes run through the **forest and the abandoned old village** — the same ground [Rezeń](../characters/butcher.md) and [Ryszard Dudka](../characters/neighbour.md) know, and near the [well](../locations/the-well.md). And in a zone this thick with informing, a man who trades in secrets is a natural **SB touch-point** — another axis of leverage and danger.
