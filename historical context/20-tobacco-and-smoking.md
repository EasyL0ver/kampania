# Tobacco & Smoking — PRL, 1960s

Reference for the cigarette subgame in the [Janina whodunit](../story-facts/janina-whodunit.md): brands as class markers, cigarette butts as evidence, and how villagers would (and would not) judge a smoker. The short version: in 1967 smoking is normal, not shameful, and the only credible disapproval is moral, thrifty, or about propriety, never health.

## Smoking Was Near-Universal and Socially Accepted

In 1960s PRL smoking was one of the most common and accepted parts of daily life. Smoke was everywhere: homes, streets, offices, restaurants, trains, even hospitals and schools. Almost everyone smoked, men and women, politicians, doctors, workers, often around children. A non-smoker existed but was rare, and their objection was treated as a caprice rather than a legitimate complaint.

- The cigarette "with coffee," at gatherings, meetings, and social occasions was a fixed ritual. Shared smoking cemented acquaintance and was part of both social and working life.

## Health Harm Was Not in Public Consciousness

Information about the harms of smoking existed but was ignored or dismissed. Public knowledge of the link between smoking and lung disease or cancer was low. The state ran no anti-smoking campaigns and the topic barely appeared in media. Smoking was called an "unhealthy habit" at most, without the long-term risk being widely understood.

- **Design consequence:** a character who condemns smoking on health grounds is anachronistic. Disapproval must be framed as thrift ("a waste of money"), self-discipline ("a man who cannot govern himself"), or propriety ("not in God's house"), not as concern for the lungs.

## The Church Did Not Treat It as Sin

In the 1960s the Church ran no notable campaign against smoking and generally did not condemn it as sin; it was treated as a private matter or a minor weakness. Clergy comments on the habit were rare. More critical pastoral remarks only appear in later years, once the harm became obvious.

- **Design consequence:** [ks. Pająk](../characters/priest.md) keeping smoke out of the sanctuary reads as personal austerity and respect for sacred space, not doctrine. It is a believable public front for a man who [secretly smokes](../clues/clues.md#priest-smokes-carmen).

## Brand Was a Class and Status Marker

The better the cigarette, the more it signalled wealth or access. Dearer and Western brands (sold through Pewex) were prestige objects. Availability of any brand fluctuated with rationing and shortages, so a man who always has the expensive pack is making a statement.

- **Period brands (real):** the cheap and common **Sport** and **Popularne**, the stronger **Mocne**/**Extra Mocne**, and the pricier **Klubowe**, **Carmen**, **Karo**, **Dukaty**. Many were filterless.
- **Campaign use:** [Carmen](../items/carmen-cigarette-butts.md) as the premium brand only two men openly buy ([Junior](../characters/junior.md), [Rezeń](../characters/butcher.md)) and [Sport](../clues/clues.md#tadek-smokes-cheapest) as [Tadek](../characters/wujas.md)'s cheap brand are both historically correct as a rich-versus-poor contrast.

## Plausibility Verdict For the Campaign

- ✅ **Fully grounded:** brand as a class/wealth marker; a butt placing a person; Sport (cheap) versus Carmen (premium) as a poverty-versus-status contrast.
- ✅ **Grounded:** a proud poor man taking any free cigarette; better brands scarce and coveted.
- ⚠️ **Bend, don't lean on:** open moral disapproval of smoking itself. A non-smoker is fine, but frame the judgment as thrift, discipline, or propriety, and expect the village to treat the objection as odd.
- ❌ **Avoid:** any health-based or anti-smoking-campaign framing, or the Church condemning tobacco as sin. Both are anachronistic for 1967.
