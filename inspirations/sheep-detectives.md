# The Sheep Detectives (2026)

**Type:** Structure / tone inspiration

## Overview

A flock of sheep in an English village set out to solve the murder of their shepherd, George, who used to read them detective novels every night. Led by the clever ewe Lily, they investigate the humans of the village using everything they overheard from the stories. A whodunnit built on outsiders piecing together a closed community's secrets. Based on Leonie Swann's novel *Three Bags Full*.

## Atmosphere & Themes

- **Outsiders reading a closed village.** The investigators don't belong and don't fully understand the human world — they assemble the truth from fragments, overheard talk, and behaviour that the villagers think no one is watching. Mirrors the committee: government clerks who don't belong, decoding a village that closes ranks.
- **A death that unlocks the community's secrets.** One killing exposes that everyone had a reason, a grudge, a thing they were hiding. The murder is the door; the village is the mystery.
- **Investigation from observation, not force.** The detectives can't interrogate or coerce. They watch, listen, and notice — the whole case is built from opportunities, not confrontations.
- **Gentle surface, real stakes underneath.** Whimsy on top, genuine grief and betrayal beneath. A tonal counterweight worth remembering when the Bieszczady material gets relentlessly grim.
- **The Christie formula as a logical puzzle.** The film runs on classic golden-age structure: for each suspect the sheep work out **alibi, motive, and means**, and cross a name off when one of the three fails. The case is a solvable grid, not a mood. A clean model for structuring the committee's suspects — every clue slots into one of those three columns, and the players win by filling the grid.
- **Knowing the genre from the inside.** The sheep solve the case because they know how murder mysteries are supposed to go. A nod to players who metagame the whodunnit — and to how expectation shapes what an investigator sees.
