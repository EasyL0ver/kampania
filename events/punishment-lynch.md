# The Lynch

**Type:** Event — endgame catastrophe
**Location:** [%NEW_VILLAGE%](../locations/old-village-ruins.md) — the road and yards after dark, ending at [the well](../locations/the-well.md) in %OLD_VILLAGE%
**Present:** [Ryszard Dudka](../characters/neighbour.md) at the front, rifle in hand; men from [Tadek's](../characters/wujas.md) drinking circle, drunk and purposeful; [Barbara](../characters/barbara.md) **only if she was never warned** ([see below](#barbara)); a target set by [Dudka's targeting score](../characters/neighbour.md#lynch-targets)
**Available:** **Night of Day 6.** This is not optional and it is not preventable in full. The valley's reflex fires. **Someone goes into the well tonight.** What the players decide is *who* — and what the killing is wrapped in.

## Trigger

Thirteen years of listening through the wall and doing nothing. The flood, the well, the dreams, and now a fresh body already taken to the water — it turns [Dudka's](../characters/neighbour.md) guilt into something he can no longer lie still under, and it comes out as a rifle, not a confession. He gathers the men who are already angry and the valley does what it did in 1954.

This is the opening blow of the endgame, not its close. The body drops tonight; the [village's reckoning with its dead](the-ritual.md) and its [reckoning with the flood](the-flood.md) both play out *after*, in the morning that follows, in the shadow of who the well just took.

## Hook

For a day beforehand the drinking circle gets louder and turns from miserable to purposeful. If a player [was warned by Barbara](barbara-warns-the-players.md), they saw this coming and have the evening to prepare. If not: one night, with no warning — lamps moving on the road, boots on gravel, dogs going off down the valley, [Dudka's](../characters/neighbour.md) rifle gone from its pegs. Perceivable anywhere in %NEW_VILLAGE% after dark.

## Setup

A knot of men with lamps and bottles, no longer slumped — moving with intent. Dudka at the front, rifle across him, quiet and white-faced (he goes quieter, not louder, when it's real). Someone is already being dragged along or barricaded against — the target. The valley has the muscle memory: the same darkness, the same drink, the same well at the end of the road. No phone, no road out, no witness who'll talk.

His state colours the night:
- **If he acted at [the well](well-confrontation.md) already** (held Rezeń at rifle-point): harder and more certain — he's crossed into *doing* once, and this is the next step.
- **If [Humiliated](../characters/neighbour.md#humiliated):** colder, past words, out to prove he isn't nothing. Hardest to turn.
- **If a player holds his [bond](../characters/neighbour.md#bond):** he clocks that player in the lamplight — the one voice that can still reach him.

### Barbara

- **If Barbara was [warned and sent home](barbara-warns-the-players.md):** she is not here. The mob is drunk men and a rifle — breakable.
- **If no player ever bonded with her:** **she is standing at Dudka's shoulder.** Not chanting, not pointing — miserable, arms crossed, eyes down, but *present.* She won't cross the man who protected her for the people who never protected her. Her presence gives the mob what drink can't: **legitimacy.** With Barbara there, "you're just drunk, go home" doesn't work — the kindest soul in the valley is standing with them, and everyone can see it. Turning the mob is far harder while she stays.

**Composure: 2**

## Opportunities

- **The front of the mob** — **Observation:** a handful of men, not the village; most are drunk followers who'd fold if the nerve went out of them. Only Dudka is driving it — unless [Barbara](#barbara) is there lending weight.
- **The target** — **Read / Milicjant:** whether they've got a guilty man (Rezeń, a sibling) or the wrong one (Marek, Barbara, the hag, a player) — and that to the mob it makes no difference.
- **Dudka's face** — **Read:** not bloodlust — a man who cannot stand his own inaction one more night. He isn't doing this because he's sure. He's doing it because doing *nothing* is finally worse.
- **The script** — **History / Culture:** this is 1954 running again, beat for beat — same drink, same dark, same well. The valley is not choosing. It is repeating.

## Actions

**The killing cannot be called off — only aimed, or absorbed.** Every action below changes *who* goes into the well and what the death means. None of them sends everyone home.

### Turn the aim onto a perpetrator
- **Requires:** proof that points at one of the guilty (Rezeń, a sibling, Helena)
- **Cost:** —
- **Outcome:** Hand the crowd the name it half-wants and it carries that name to the well. **This feels like justice and is 1954 again** — a guilty person killed by a drunk mob in the dark, the dead still unnamed, the reflex proven eternal. If the truth was only half-out, you may be wrong about who's guilty.
- **World State Change:** the lynch completes on a perpetrator. Colours the [morning after](#aftermath) as grim vindication.

### Turn the aim off an innocent
- **Requires:** the current target ([score](../characters/neighbour.md#lynch-targets)) is the wrong person, and you have a *different* name with weight behind it
- **Cost:** 1 composure — you're arguing a drunk crowd off a kill
- **Outcome:** You pull the mob off the innocent it was fixed on by giving it someone else. You have saved one life by choosing another. The well takes a body regardless; you decided which.
- **World State Change:** the target shifts. You own the choice.

### Put yourself between them and the target
- **Requires:** a player physically steps in front of the rifle
- **Cost:** grave — **the mob may take the player instead** ([players are a valid target](../characters/neighbour.md#lynch-targets); a score high enough, or a drunk crowd denied its kill, can turn on the one blocking it)
- **Outcome:** You can save the target with your body. Whether the crowd backs down or **puts the player in the well instead** depends on Dudka's state, the player's standing, and whether [Barbara](#barbara) is there to stop it going that far. This is how a player dies tonight.
- **World State Change:** target saved; a player may be killed in their place.

### Convince Dudka justice will be delivered
- **Requires:** you hold [his bond](../characters/neighbour.md#bond) **or** he isn't [Humiliated](../characters/neighbour.md#humiliated) **or** you've [Uplifted him](../characters/neighbour.md#uplift-ryszard) — **and** you can *prove* the guilty will answer: the truth documented, word already down the wire, an authority that will act on it
- **Cost:** 1 composure — stepping in front of a loaded rifle
- **Outcome:** Dudka isn't after blood. He's after the certainty that these men will *finally answer* — because in thirteen years no one ever made them. He lynches only because he believes the alternative is nothing. **Show him the alternative is real — that justice is already moving and will land — and he stands down.** He lowers the rifle and says what he's carried for thirteen years, out loud, to the committee, on the record. The truth gets out.

  **And then the mob he raised takes him.** Drunk, denied its target, and looking at the man who just broke ranks and sided with the outsiders — the crowd turns. The redeemer becomes the body. **[Dudka](../characters/neighbour.md) goes into the well tonight, in the target's place** — the coward who did nothing for thirteen years, killed the one time he did the right thing. His testimony survives him; he does not.
- **Requires (to even attempt):** the justice structure built *before* tonight, not improvised at the well. Without real proof that the guilty will answer, the words bounce off and the rifle stays up.
- **World State Change:** the intended target lives; the truth is on the record ([toward Justice](../story-facts/game-system.md#endings)); **Dudka is dead.** This is the narrow exit — and it still costs a life. Just his.

### Let it run
- **Requires:** —
- **Cost:** —
- **Outcome:** You don't step in. The mob goes to the well and the [targeting score](../characters/neighbour.md#lynch-targets) decides who. See [Aftermath](#aftermath).

## Aftermath

Unless [Dudka was convinced justice is coming](#convince-dudka-justice-will-be-delivered), **the lynch completes and a body goes into the well.** Who, is decided by [Dudka's targeting score](../characters/neighbour.md#lynch-targets) — the person the players' own leaks (mostly through [Barbara's fence](../characters/barbara.md)) made look guiltiest.

- **A perpetrator** (Rezeń, a sibling, Helena): the village calls it justice; it is the same crime, thirteen years on.
- **The wrong person** (Marek, Barbara, the hag): the committee's carelessness put an innocent in the well.
- **A player:** the outsiders who came to judge became the guiltiest-looking thing in the valley — or one of them stepped in front of the rifle.
- **[Dudka himself](#convince-dudka-justice-will-be-delivered):** if the players convinced him justice was coming, he stood down, testified — and his own mob killed him for it. The truth is on the record; the coward died the one time he did right.

Afterward [Dudka](../characters/neighbour.md) is hollowed out, not relieved — he has become the thing he spent thirteen years hating. The well takes another body, and [what that does](../story-facts/well-influence.md) follows.

**And then it is morning, and the endgame is not over.** The lynch is the wound the last day is lived inside. Whoever is left faces both reckonings at once — the [dead](the-ritual.md) and the [flood](the-flood.md) — one day, too few hands, and a fresh body in the well changing the weight of every choice. If the lynch killed a player, the party enters that final day short-handed. What the mob took, it took from the living who still have to choose.
