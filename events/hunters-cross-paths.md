# The Hunters Cross Paths

**Location:** Forest between the villages
**Present:** [Ryszard Dudka](../characters/neighbour.md) (rifle), [Stanisław Rezeń](../characters/butcher.md) (three dogs), players (with whichever hunter they followed)
**Available:** During either [The Hunt with Rezeń](hunt-with-rezen.md) or [The Hunt with Dudka](hunt-with-dudka.md) — both men are working the same pack through the same woods on the same day, and it comes to a head the moment Dudka finally has a shot and Rezeń's dogs blow it.

## Trigger

Both men have been closing on the same pack all day. It comes to a head when [Ryszard Dudka](../characters/neighbour.md) finally gets his chance — bellied down in the brush, rifle up, the wolves grazing a clearing below him, the shot he's needed for weeks lined up at last. And that is the moment [Stanisław Rezeń](../characters/butcher.md)'s dogs come crashing through.

## Setup

Dudka is stone-still in the ferns, cheek to the stock, breath held. Below him the pack moves in the open — close, unaware, his at last. His finger is taking up the slack.

Then the brush erupts. [Rezeń](../characters/butcher.md)'s three dogs come tearing through downwind, driving straight at the wolves, and the clearing detonates — wolves scattering into the trees, dogs baying after them, the whole day's work gone in three seconds. Dudka's shot goes wide into empty air, or never comes at all.

He comes up out of the ferns roaring. Weeks of failure, the wojewoda's public dressing-down, the shot finally his — and *this man* walked it out of his hands. He gets in Rezeń's face, rifle gripped white-knuckled across his chest, screaming him down: a butcher, a murderer, a thing that should never have been let back among people. [Rezeń](../characters/butcher.md) stops. He doesn't shout back — he goes very still and very quiet, and the knife is already in his hand, turning. The three dogs peel off the wolf-line and fan out around him, low and silent, hackles up, waiting for a word that hasn't come yet.

For a few seconds it is entirely real: a shaking man with a rifle, a calm man with a knife and three dogs, and nothing between them but air. One wrong movement and someone bleeds out under the trees, and the road is flooded, and no one is coming.

## Opportunities

- **The standoff** — rifle against knife and dogs, both men committed, neither backing off. **Read:** Dudka is louder but Rezeń is the dangerous one — the butcher hasn't raised his voice, hasn't blinked, and the blade is already out. Whoever the players are standing next to, they can feel which man is actually ready to kill.
- **Dudka's fury** — he's screaming it plain for anyone to hear: *"That's not a hunter — that's a butcher who found a reason to carry a knife in daylight. He shouldn't be anywhere near people."* [`dudka-despises-rezen`](../clues/clues.md#dudka-despises-rezen)
- **Rezeń's cold** — **Observation / Milicjant:** he isn't angry. His breathing is even, his eyes flat, the knife moving in that idle turn even now. This is a man who has stood exactly here before and knows how it ends. [`butcher-is-dangerous`](../clues/clues.md#butcher-is-dangerous)
- **The dogs** — they move on him like limbs, no commands spoken. **Read:** if this goes wrong, it won't be a fair fight; it'll be three dogs and a blade against one furious old man. [`butcher-is-dangerous`](../clues/clues.md#butcher-is-dangerous)

## Actions

### Get between them
- **Requires:** A player willing to step into the gap
- **Cost:** 1 action
- **Outcome:** Someone has to break it, because neither of these men will. Stepping in physically or talking them down both work — this is a no-failure moment, so the standoff ends without blood the instant a player commits to ending it. How it ends colors what they earn:
  - **Command / Milicjant:** Pull rank — committee authority, a barked order. Both men stand down to the outsiders because neither wants a government witness to what almost happened. Rezeń pockets the knife first, which tells the players something: he was never out of control.
  - **Sweettalk:** Talk Dudka down. He lets the players walk him back, still shaking. Rezeń simply leaves the moment the rifle drops — no parting word. Dudka, spent, gives up more than he means to about *why* the hate runs this deep (leverage — see [Ryszard Dudka](../characters/neighbour.md)).
  - **Intimidation / Strength:** Physically separate them. Rezeń yields ground without argument; Dudka has to be held. Both note who did it.
- **Gives:** World State Change — the two men are pulled apart with no one hurt; **Gives:** [`butcher-is-dangerous`](../clues/clues.md#butcher-is-dangerous)

### Let it burn
- **Requires:** The players choose not to intervene
- **Cost:** Free
- **Outcome:** Nobody has to defuse it, because Rezeń defuses it himself — by winning. He lets Dudka scream himself hoarse, lets the rifle shake, and then he *laughs* — a low, genuine, delighted laugh, the first warm sound anyone's heard out of him, and it's the cruelest thing he could do. He nods at the rifle in Dudka's white-knuckled hands: *"You had your shot. Not at the wolves — at me. Clear as day. And you didn't take it."* The laugh again, soft. *"You never do, Ryszard. You point, you shake, you shout. You don't have it in you and you never did."* Dudka has no answer. He lowers the rifle, gray in the face, and Rezeń whistles his dogs up and strolls off, unhurried, the clear winner of a fight he never raised his voice in.
  - **Read:** Rezeń didn't just avoid a fight — he *enjoyed* it. Domination is a pleasure to him, and he just took Dudka apart without a blow. And the needle only works because it's true of *him* in reverse: he said *you don't have it in you* like a man who knows exactly what it takes, because he does.
- **Gives:** [`butcher-is-dangerous`](../clues/clues.md#butcher-is-dangerous) (the taunt lands because Rezeń plainly has the thing Dudka lacks); [`dudka-despises-rezen`](../clues/clues.md#dudka-despises-rezen); **Ending Progress** — the humiliation drives Dudka harder toward the [lynch ending](punishment-lynch.md).

## If Missed

If the players aren't in the forest, the dogs still blow Dudka's shot and the two men still square off — rifle against knife and dogs. It ends the way it ends when no one intervenes: Rezeń laughs Dudka down, mocks him for never having the nerve to pull the trigger, and walks off unhurried. Dudka carries the humiliation home, and it goes straight onto the fire that pushes toward the [lynch](punishment-lynch.md). The players just don't witness it.
