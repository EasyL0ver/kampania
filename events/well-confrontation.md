# The Well Confrontation

**Type:** Event — climactic, one-shot (Night, Day 4)
**Location:** [%OLD_VILLAGE%](../locations/old-village-ruins.md) — the well
**Present:** [Paraskewia Chyłak](../characters/hag.md) (performing her rites), [Stanisław Rezeń](../characters/butcher.md) (arrives with a dead wolf, three dogs). [Ryszard Dudka](../characters/neighbour.md) **only if** the players got him here *and* left him his rifle. Players only if they invested in being here.
**Available:** Night of Day 4 — once [Rezeń's containment has broken](wolf-attack.md) (the wolf authorization) and [Paraskewia](../characters/hag.md) is at the well for her nightly rites. Players don't stumble in; being present takes one of:
- **Stake out the well** — they know [Rezeń goes there at night](../clues/clues.md#rezen-leaves-at-night) and choose to watch (1 night action).
- **Follow Rezeń** — they tail him from his house down the forest path, dogs at his heels (1 night action).
- **Already at the ruins at night** — investigating for their own reasons; they hear the chanting first, then the footsteps.
- **With Dudka** — see the *Dudka's rifle* action; getting him here is set up beforehand (below).
**Cost:** 1 night action to be present

## Trigger

Three things converge this night. [Rezeń](../characters/butcher.md) is loose — the wolf authorization broke thirteen years of containment and the well's compulsion is screaming; he goes to the ruins every night now. [Paraskewia](../characters/hag.md) is at the well, as she has been for twenty years, praying for the dead. And the village wants her gone — weeks of wolf attacks blamed on her rites ([hag-blamed-for-wolves](../clues/clues.md#hag-blamed-for-wolves)) mean nobody is protecting the witch. Whatever happens tonight, nobody will ask questions.

## Hook

From the ruins at night: chanting carried across the well clearing, low and rhythmic. Players following Rezeń instead hear dogs and a man's unhurried footsteps on the forest path ahead.

## Setup

The well clearing at night. [Paraskewia](../characters/hag.md) kneels at the well's rim inside a semicircle of real beeswax candles. An icon of the Theotokos is propped against the stone, face turned to the water; bread on a cloth, a bowl of water and herbs, real church incense. She is chanting — Lemko, low and steady, not a performance but a conversation twenty years deep. The words aren't meant to be understood; the cadence is unmistakable prayer. The clearing smells of incense and wet stone and something older underneath. Even the wolves are elsewhere tonight.

Then he comes up the village path, three dogs trotting ahead, a dead wolf limp over his shoulder — the same routine, kill a wolf, bring it to the well, feed the hole. He sees her and stops. He's *friendly*: the performed warmth he's been wearing at the PGR lately. *"Go home, granny. It's cold."* He doesn't recognize her — just an old woman at his well, in his way. She recognizes him: the auxiliary who leaned into the killing when the soldiers came in 1947, the footprints she's found at this well for twenty years. She doesn't move. She turns back to the water and prays louder.

The friendliness curdles into play. He drops the wolf, circles her, jokes — *"The village says you send the wolves. Here, I brought you one back."* — the knife turning idle in his hand, the dogs pacing. He's testing her, enjoying the power, in no hurry. She won't flinch, won't look at him, won't give him the fear he came to enjoy. She treats him like weather. That's what flips it: the playfulness dies into something quieter. He sets the knife on the rim — he won't need it for her — and steps in.

**[GM — turn to the players now. Whatever they do happens here, and fast.]** If no one stops him it is neither loud nor long: a short struggle, the dogs, then quiet. He lifts her over the rim, drops the wolf in after her, stands a moment — calm, content the way a man is when an itch finally stops — then whistles the dogs and walks back down the path.

## Opportunities

- **The rites** — candles, icon, bread, incense, the shape of the chant. **Observation:** liturgical, not improvised — a Lemko woman praying for her dead in the only way she knows. Not witchcraft, not summoning. [`hag-tends-the-well`](../clues/clues.md#hag-tends-the-well)
- **The dead wolf on his shoulder** — he carried it here for a reason. **Read:** this is a routine; he's fed this hole before, with carcasses, with bones. [`butcher-compelled-to-feed`](../clues/clues.md#butcher-compelled-to-feed), [`butcher-visits-the-well`](../clues/clues.md#butcher-visits-the-well)
- **She isn't afraid** — she knew someone would come, and stayed. **Read:** whatever this well holds, tending it matters to her more than her own life.
- **Rezeń at play** — calm, smiling, the knife turning, in no rush. **Read / Milicjant:** this is a practiced, controlled man who *enjoys* it — never more himself than when someone else should be afraid. [`butcher-is-dangerous`](../clues/clues.md#butcher-is-dangerous)
- **The ambivalence** — **Read:** is this the well's compulsion, or just a man who came to dump a wolf, found an obstacle, and discovered he liked removing it? He was friendly; he joked; when she wouldn't give him fear he took the rest. Both readings are true and neither is wrong.

## Actions

The players are census bureaucrats; Rezeń has a knife, three dogs, and twenty years of practice. Unarmed, none of this stops him for good — **unless the party has a fighter *and* the hands to spare on his dogs.** One person can't do both. The options escalate; a player can climb from one to the next as the moment slips away.

### Grapple him
- **Requires:** Present, no firearm
- **Cost:** 1 composure
- **Outcome:** You shove him, try to drag her clear. He throws you off, the dogs snap at ankles — bruised and shaken, not wounded. **World State Change:** she's still alive when he resumes; you bought her seconds, nothing more. A clerk's hands aren't enough.

### Grab the hag and run
- **Requires:** Present, no firearm, Endurance
- **Cost:** 1 composure; causes a Wound
- **Outcome:** You scoop her up and crash into the dark. The dogs give chase and bite; the terrain mauls you both. **You are wounded.** Rezeń doesn't follow — the well, unattended, is what he wants. **World State Change:** the hag survives but is badly hurt, and whether she can keep up the rites is an open question. (Because being wounded blocks Endurance, you can only do this once.)

### Fight him
- **Requires:** Present, no firearm, **Violence** — **and at least one other player committing to hold off the dogs.** One fighter cannot do both; the three dogs are what make Rezeń more than a man with a knife.
- **Cost:** 2 composure from the fighter; 1 composure from each helper on the dogs; **the fighter is wounded, and any helper who takes the dogs head-on may be too** (GM's call by how they do it)
- **Outcome:** This only works as a team. While one or more players keep the dogs off — clubs, coats thrown over heads, backs to the well, taking bites to keep the jaws busy — the fighter gets Rezeń one-on-one and goes at him with intent. You meet the knife and give back worse. Rezeń came to remove an obstacle, not to bleed for one; when the obstacle fights like it means it *and* his dogs are tied up, the arithmetic changes. It's ugly and it costs blood (**the fighter is wounded** — see [Wounds](../story-facts/game-system.md#wounds)), but you drive him off the well and he backs into the trees, whistling the dogs after him. **The hag survives.** And you've made yourselves his: he has your faces now, and a man like Rezeń settles debts on his own time. **World State Change / Ending Progress** — you are marked as the ones who stood up to the butcher, which cuts both ways.
  - **Solo (Violence, no help):** Don't. Without anyone on the dogs, one fighter can trade with Rezeń *or* survive the pack, not both. The dogs take you off your feet, the knife does the rest — you're wounded for nothing, and worse, you're down while he finishes what he came to do. **The hag dies anyway.** This is the lesson the scene wants: alone, you are not enough.
  - **Milicjant:** You fight like it's on duty — controlled, aiming to disable, going for the knife hand. You *could* try to pin him for [por. Skowron](../characters/officer.md) — but you already know how that ends: no cuffs, no cell, no charge that sticks yet, and the moment your friends let the dogs go he's gone. You hold him off; you don't hold him. Better to know that now than to learn it with his knife in you.
  - **Intimidate:** As he retreats you make it plain this isn't over — and he *laughs*, delighted, filing your face away like a gift. You didn't scare him. You interested him. [`butcher-is-dangerous`](../clues/clues.md#butcher-is-dangerous)

### Draw a firearm
- **Requires:** Present, holding a gun — [Dudka's rifle](../characters/neighbour.md) (borrowed/taken) or [Zbigniew's pistol](../characters/wojewoda.md) (from his office). Pointing it is free.
- **Cost:** 2 composure to pull the trigger — but see below
- **Outcome:** The scene changes completely. **The GM asks: *"Are you going to shoot him? It costs 2 composure."*** The player must answer before anything continues.
  - **Yes, with composure to back it:** They spend **1**, not 2 — the GM was bluffing. Rezeń reads the resolve, lowers the knife, raises his palms — *"Easy. Easy."* — and walks backward into the tree line, dogs following. **The hag survives.** Then the real question, as he retreats: **the GM asks *"He's leaving. Do you shoot him in the back? 2 composure."*** Spend it and he goes down — a committee member has executed a retreating man, and [por. Witold Skowron](../characters/officer.md) and [Zbigniew](../characters/wojewoda.md) will both want answers (**Ending Progress** — a path to the truth bought with murder). Refuse and he vanishes; the hag lives; you know exactly what he is, and he's still out there.
  - **Yes, but no composure to spend:** *"Your hands are shaking. You know you can't."* Rezeń calls the bluff and keeps going.
  - **No:** He reads the hesitation and keeps going. You watch, and learn something about yourself.

### Dudka's rifle (if he is present)
- **Requires:** The players got Dudka here **and** left him his rifle. He comes if they fed him what he needed beforehand — any of: they followed Rezeń's night walks and mentioned it to him; they found the well and asked him for backup; or they told [Barbara](../characters/barbara.md), who tells him everything over the fence ([the sieve](../characters/barbara.md#warning)). Without the rifle he won't come — he knows what Rezeń is and won't walk in unarmed.
- **Cost:** None from the players — Dudka acts
- **Outcome:** From the trees, the sound of a rifle bolt. Dudka steps out, rifle up, aimed at Rezeń's chest, and says nothing. Unarmed bureaucrats are one thing; a hunter with a loaded rifle at twenty metres is another. Rezeń reads it, backs off, walks away, dogs following. **The hag survives.** Dudka lowers the rifle, hands shaking, staring at the well. Not redemption — the bare minimum he couldn't manage thirteen years ago. But he *moved*. **World State Change / Ending Progress:** Rezeń knows Dudka was watching, Dudka knows Rezeń was about to kill; neither can pretend anymore, and the village's silence cracks — gasoline for Day 7. (If the players aren't present, Dudka does this alone, same outcome, unwitnessed — he's quieter and harder after Day 4, and the story comes out only in fragments if pushed, or from the hag.)

## If Missed

**Default (~70%): the hag dies.** Without a push, Dudka's surveillance never leaves the tree line and the players never invest in being here, so the confrontation happens offscreen. Rezeń arrives, finds her, kills her — the same practiced motion as 1954 — and drops another body in the well.

The players discover the aftermath on Day 5+ when they next reach the well: ritual supplies scattered and trampled, an icon face-down, blood smeared on the stone rim, drag marks from a kneeling position to the edge, the well uncovered and silent. The immediately visible fact: someone was killed at the well while performing the rites ([`someone-killed-at-well`](../clues/clues.md#someone-killed-at-well)). Deeper investigation — the water, the tracks, the cabin — runs through [The Well](../locations/the-well.md#actions) and [Hag's Cabin](../locations/hags-cabin.md#actions). Ask the village and nobody cares: *"Good riddance — she was sending the wolves"* ([`hag-blamed-for-wolves`](../clues/clues.md#hag-blamed-for-wolves)); the one exception is [Dudka](../characters/neighbour.md), who quietly insists she was just an old woman.

**World State Change — the well is uncontained.** Her twenty years of rites were the only thing holding the influence in check, and now her body is *in* it. Dreams turn unbearable and reach beyond the ruins; other villagers ([Janina](../characters/ciotka.md), [Irena](../characters/wife.md), [Tadek](../characters/wujas.md), even [Zbigniew](../characters/wojewoda.md)) begin to dream. Rezeń accelerates — killing feeds the compulsion rather than sating it, and the cold methodical man becomes something rawer. The rites can't be resumed unless the players reconstruct [the ritual](../story-facts/the-ritual.md) themselves.
