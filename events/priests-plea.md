# The Priest's Plea

**Location:** [The church](../locations/the-church.md) — the rectory beside it, after hours
**Present:** [ks. Władysław Pająk](../characters/priest.md) (shaken, sober — this is before the breaking), and **one** player: the one who has shown him genuine faith. He asks for that player alone.
**Available:** Day 4 onward, after [Holy Mass](holy-mass.md). Triggers once a player has shown ks. Pająk real faith — progressed his [bond](../characters/priest.md#bond): asked his counsel, confided in him, or honoured the church. He seeks that player out; he will not open this to a whole committee.

## Trigger

The priest has spent thirteen years holding the village's sins behind the seal and telling himself the water would never come. Now it has. He needs to say the fear out loud to someone, and he cannot say it to a parishioner — they'd carry it back into the village. A pious outsider is the only safe confessor he has. So he reaches for the one player who showed him something real.

## Hook

A quiet word after Mass, or [Krystian](../characters/secondary-characters.md) sent with a folded note, or the priest himself at the door where the committee is billeted: *would you come and sit with me a while.* Not urgent on its face. But he doesn't ask twice, and he doesn't ask anyone else.

## Setup

The rectory is small, book-lined, colder than the church. One lamp. Rain on the glass. He pours tea he doesn't drink and sits across from the player with his hands folded, and for a long moment he is just a tired man in a black cassock who cannot find the first sentence.

Then he finds it. He is worried, he says, that the flood is not an accident of engineering. He is worried it is **judgment** — that God has looked at this valley and decided to wash it away, the way He washed the world in Noah's day, the way He turned on Sodom. He does not say what sin. He says only that a place can carry a wrong so long and so quietly that heaven finally answers it with water. And he asks the player — not as a priest, he has no answers left, but as one believer to another — *do you think a man who has done something unforgivable can still be forgiven?*

He is asking about someone specific. He will not say who. He may be asking about himself.

**Composure: 0** — restores 1 for a player of faith; being trusted by him steadies you as much as it weighs on you.

## Opportunities

- **The man, not the office** — he has taken off the professional calm he wears at the plebania door.
  - **Read:** This isn't a sermon and it isn't a test. He is frightened, and he has come to a layperson for reassurance a priest is supposed to give, not receive. That reversal is how far he's already fallen.

- **The scripture he reaches for** — Noah, the Deluge, Sodom, the cities under the sea.
  - **Culture / History:** Every example is a *judgment* narrative — God destroying the wicked by water or fire. Not one is about mercy. His mind is running down one track, and it ends with the valley deserving to drown.

- **The sin he won't name** — he keeps circling a specific wrong without touching it.
  - **Read:** He isn't speaking in general. There is a particular sin, and he knows exactly what it is.
  - **If the player holds [`priest-knows-everything`](../clues/clues.md#priest-knows-everything):** you can feel the shape of it under his words — the lynch, the well, the thing thirteen years of confession put in his keeping and the seal won't let him say. He is grieving a truth he cannot report.

- **What's holding it in** — his hand keeps going to his collar, to the white line of the office, and coming away.
  - **Read:** The vow is the only wall between what he knows and what he'd say — and he isn't standing behind that wall so much as leaning on it, exhausted. A man this tired doesn't guard a secret forever. He waits for a reason to set it down.
  - **If the player holds [`priest-knows-everything`](../clues/clues.md#priest-knows-everything):** the implication is quietly enormous. Everything is behind that wall. He will not let go tonight — but a person watching closely can see that he *could*, and that whatever broke him would bring all of it out with him.

## Actions

### Tell him people can be forgiven — back his faith
- **Requires:** The player answers his question toward **mercy** — that no one is beyond redemption, that God's forgiveness is larger than any sin
- **Cost:** Free
- **Outcome:** Something in him steadies. It is the first time in weeks anyone has offered him the merciful reading instead of the fearful one, and it comes from someone whose faith he already trusts. He doesn't decide anything tonight — but the track his mind was running down forks, and mercy is now on the map. This is the seed of the whole [Grace](../story-facts/spiritual-endings.md) path.
  - **NPC State Change:** the Grace arc opens — ks. Pająk is now a priest who *might* reach for mercy rather than accept judgment.
  - **Ending Progress:** **+2 to the [Faith in Redemption](../story-facts/spiritual-endings.md) score** (backing his faith).
  - **Gives:** → [priest-fears-divine-judgment](../clues/clues.md#priest-fears-divine-judgment)

### Ask him what he needs
- **Requires:** Nothing
- **Cost:** Free
- **Outcome:** He answers plainly, and it is the closest he can come to naming the task without breaking the seal: bring the lost back to God before the water comes — *especially the ones with the most to answer for.* A soul that drowns confessed drowns saved; a soul that drowns hard drowns damned. He is telling the players, in the only language he's allowed, how [Grace](../story-facts/spiritual-endings.md) is won: get the guilty to his confessional.
  - **NPC State Change:** the players now know, in-character, what tips him toward the odpust — get the guilty to confess.
  - **Gives:** → [priest-fears-divine-judgment](../clues/clues.md#priest-fears-divine-judgment)

### Push him to name the sin / break the seal
- **Requires:** The player presses him to say what he knows — whose sin, what happened
- **Cost:** —
- **Outcome:** The office snaps back on like a door closing. He will not, cannot, betray the confessional — and the fact that a person he trusted reached straight for it wounds him. He withdraws, thanks the player stiffly, and ends the evening. He has learned this player wants the secret more than the soul.
  - **NPC State Change:** the bond cools; he is warier with the committee from here.
  - **Ending Progress:** **−2 to the [Faith in Redemption](../story-facts/spiritual-endings.md) score** (pushing him to weaponise confession).

### Tell him the valley deserves it — agree it's judgment
- **Requires:** The player answers his question toward **condemnation** — that some things can't be forgiven, that the guilty have it coming
- **Cost:** —
- **Outcome:** He nods slowly, as if a suspicion has been confirmed by someone he respects. It is what half of him already believes. His faith settles harder onto the judgment track — and a priest who has decided the valley earned the water grants no odpust.
  - **NPC State Change:** ks. Pająk leans toward judgment; the Grace path narrows.
  - **Ending Progress:** **−2 to the [Faith in Redemption](../story-facts/spiritual-endings.md) score** (feeding judgment).
  - **Gives:** → [priest-fears-divine-judgment](../clues/clues.md#priest-fears-divine-judgment)

## If Missed

If no player ever earns his trust, the plea never comes. ks. Pająk carries the fear alone, no one offers him the merciful reading, and his crisis runs its default course toward judgment across the [second](second-flood-mass.md) and [third](third-flood-mass.md) flood masses. The Grace path can still be opened later by bringing the guilty to confess — but the players will have to find that on their own, without the priest ever telling them how.
