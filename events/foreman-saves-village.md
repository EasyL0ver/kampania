# Michał Pytlak Saves the Village — The Engineering Ending

**Location:** [UPA bunker](../locations/upa-bunker.md) (for the explosives), then the ridge above %NEW_VILLAGE% where the water-gap is plugged
**Present:** [Michał Pytlak](../characters/foreman.md), players. [Zofia Pytlak](../characters/zofia.md) circles the edges of it, trying to pull her husband out.
**Available:** Day 5 onward — after [Foreman's Flood Fight](foremans-flood-fight.md), once the players know the plan and agree to help. Needs the flood proof and, ideally, [flood-cause-plugged-gap](../clues/clues.md#flood-cause-plugged-gap).

## Trigger

[Michał Pytlak](../characters/foreman.md) has a way to save %NEW_VILLAGE%. Not sandbags. Not prayers. **He means to reopen the water-gap** — the notch in the ridge between the two valleys that an old landslide plugged years ago. Clear the plug and the floodwater drains through into the lower [old-village](../locations/old-village-ruins.md) basin where it belongs, instead of backing up onto the houses.

The only fast way to move that much slid rock is **explosives** — and the only explosives in the valley are the decades-old partisan ordnance in the [UPA bunker](../locations/upa-bunker.md) out in the forest.

It's desperate. It's dangerous. It's also, unlike everything else in this valley, *correct.*

## Hook

You can hear him before you see him — the flood line, hour after hour, one man setting a pace no fifty-seven-year-old body should hold. Workers say he hasn't been home since the water rose. And now he's stopped hauling sand and started asking a different question: *where's the old bunker, and what's still in it?*

## Setup

Pytlak lays it out flat, the way he lays out everything. The village is drowning for one stupid reason. The water wants to run off into the low ground — the old-village valley, the natural sink, next door and lower. It always did. But the ridge between the two valleys has a gap in it, and years back a slice of hillside came down and choked that gap with loose rock and earth. *"The hill fell in the door,"* he says. Now the water can't get through, so it climbs the wrong way, into the houses.

He's right, and a survey proves it (see [flood-cause-plugged-gap](../clues/clues.md#flood-cause-plugged-gap)). He knew it by instinct before any government man with instruments turned up — he's read this ground for thirty years. Nobody with authority ever listened.

The plan is two hard problems:

1. **Get the explosives.** The [UPA bunker](../locations/upa-bunker.md) holds partisan ordnance from the 1940s — shells, ammunition, maybe demolition charges. It's partially collapsed, flooded in sections, rotten-timbered. The ordnance is old: it hasn't gone weak, it's gone **touchy** — corroded casings, degraded filler, more likely to go off from a knock or heat than when it was made. Getting usable charges out without dying is the first gamble.
2. **Crack the plug.** He doesn't have to blow the whole ridge apart — that's bedrock, no one could. He only has to open a breach in the **landslide plug**, which is loose debris and soft shale, not hard sandstone. One good charge in the right seam starts a notch; the flood does the rest, scouring the gap wide on its own. Place it wrong and you bring more hillside down, or open it too slow to matter.

The question the players actually face: do you trust a farm foreman — not an engineer, not a sapper, and right now not quite himself — carrying live partisan explosives up a saturated slope in a storm? And do you help him carry them?

## Opportunities

- **The plan is sound** — walk the ground with him and it holds up. The gap, the plug, the low basin beyond, the line the water wants to take.
  - **Engineering/Geology:** He's right on every count — the plug is what dooms the village, the debris is breachable, the flood will widen a breach itself. This is the one plan in the valley that actually works. (If a player ran the survey, this is the same feature they measured.)
- **The ordnance** — what he wants to pull out of the bunker is not safe to move.
  - **Streetwise/Military:** Twenty-year-old buried munitions don't get safer with age — they get more sensitive. Corroded fuses, unstable filler. A hard knock, a drop, a spark. Handling it is a real chance of dying before you reach the ridge.
- **Pytlak's state** — the man doing this isn't sleeping, isn't eating, isn't feeling the cold or his own bad knee (see [Foreman's Flood Fight](foremans-flood-fight.md)).
  - **Medicine:** What's keeping him upright isn't normal endurance. Whatever's driving him will drive him until his body fails — and he'll carry unstable explosives the whole way.
- **Zofia at the edges** — she isn't trying to save the farm. She's trying to save *him*, and she knows helping his plan may cost his life.
  - **Read:** She's asking the players to weigh the village against her husband, and she knows exactly what she's asking.

## Actions

### Believe him — commit to the plan
- **Requires:** Having walked the plan or holding the survey proof
- **Cost:** 1 action
- **Outcome:** The players decide it isn't suicide — it's the one real chance. Pytlak doesn't thank them; he just moves faster. From here the engineering ending is live and the village can actually be saved.
- **Scene Unlock:** The bunker run and the detonation become available as the flood's climax.

### Get the explosives from the bunker
- **Requires:** Committed to the plan; access to the [UPA bunker](../locations/upa-bunker.md)
- **Cost:** 1 action (plus the risk)
- **Outcome:** Into the collapsing, flooded dugout to find ordnance still capable of a controlled blast and carry it out without setting it off. Handled carefully — slow, cautious, someone who knows what they're touching — they come out with usable charges. Handled fast or carelessly, the touchy old filler is a real chance of a death on the way out. This is the scene's hard physical gamble.
  - **Item / Evidence:** Usable partisan demolition charges — enough to breach the plug.
- **World State Change:** The village now *has* the means to save itself. Nothing else in the valley does.

### Support the detonation — reopen the gap
- **Requires:** The charges; access to the plugged gap on the ridge
- **Cost:** 1 action
- **Outcome:** Up the saturated slope in the storm to set the charge in the seam of the landslide plug, clear back, and fire it. Placed well, the blast opens a breach and the floodwater tears into it, widening the gap fast — and the level in the village valley starts, visibly, to drop. The water pours through the reopened przełom into the old-village basin. **%NEW_VILLAGE% is saved.**
  - Placed badly — wrong seam, too small, or the slope lets go — it fails or brings more hillside down. Whether the players helped place it well (Engineering, a steady hand, clearing the area) decides which.
- **World State Change / Ending Progress:** The engineering ending resolves — the new village lives. The **old-village basin floods** as the water arrives — which it does regardless, from this flood and from the reservoir that will bury it forever. The buildings are saved; nothing about the lie the state told the village is undone by it.

## If Missed

If the players never back the plan, Pytlak does not let it go. He goes for the bunker and the ridge **alone** — a man who can't stop, carrying unstable explosives up a breaking slope with no one to clear the area or place the charge with him. Two ways it can land, GM's call by tone and timing:

- **He dies doing it** — the ordnance, the slope, or his own failing body. The gap may open anyway, half-finished, or not at all. Zofia is left with what she feared from the start.
- **He does it, and it costs him everything** — the village is saved by a man nobody helped, and he doesn't come back down the same, if he comes down at all.

Either way the players learn afterward what one stubborn farmer understood about this valley that the whole state apparatus, with its maps and its committee, never bothered to.
