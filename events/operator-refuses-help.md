# Operator Refuses Help

**Location:** %NEW_VILLAGE% — [Zbigniew Gajda's office](../locations/wojewodas-house.md), on the phone
**Present:** [%OPERATOR%](../characters/secondary-characters.md#operator-the-telephone-exchange-operator) (voice only, on the line). [Zbigniew Gajda](../characters/wojewoda.md) may be standing nearby.
**Available:** Day 3 onward — during [the flood](the-flood.md), the first time players pick up the phone to beg the outside world for rescue.

## Trigger

The village is cut off. Players lift the receiver in Zbigniew's office and ask the exchange to connect them to help — the powiat, the militia, anyone who can send a boat or a truck. Every one of those calls routes through the same town exchange, and the same flat woman's voice that has patched every call all game.

## Setup

She answers on the first ring. Clear. *Clearer than the line has been all game* — no crackle, no cutting out, as if the storm that took the road can't touch this one wire.

The players tell her everything. The road is gone. The bridge is under. People will die. They need someone to come.

And she stays on the line.

She does not refuse. She does not hang up. She answers — every plea, promptly, courteously, in the clipped grammar of the switchboard: *"Go ahead." "I've noted your request." "Remain by the receiver." "Someone will be informed."* Calm. Unhurried. Obscenely composed against their panic. They escalate; she matches every word with more procedure. Nothing she says will move a single person one step toward the valley — and she will say it, patiently, for as long as they keep talking.

A dead phone can't stay *calm at you.* This one does. It stays, and stays, and helps with nothing.

## Opportunities

- **She's a person, not a bad line** — she's lucid, present, listening. Every panicked sentence lands and gets an answer. The connection isn't failing; *she* is choosing what it carries.
  - **Read:** There's someone on the other end who has decided exactly how much she will do, and it is nothing. The calm is not indifference of a machine — it's the composure of a choice.
- **Form without substance** — listen to *what she actually promises.* Every reply is a receipt, never an action. "Noted." "Informed." "Remain by the receiver." No name, no timeline, no one dispatched.
  - **Streetwise:** This is the sound of a request being processed into a drawer. She isn't fumbling the call — she's performing the shape of help while withholding the thing itself.
- **The thing she won't do** — she never once says *no.* Refusal would be an answer, and an answer could be argued with. Instead she keeps the channel open and useless. Hanging up would be mercy; she doesn't offer it.

## Actions

### Beg her — push harder
- **Requires:** On the line with her
- **Cost:** 1 action
- **Outcome:** However they escalate — shouting, pleading, threatening, listing the children in the lower houses — she answers each one and moves nothing. The louder they get, the flatter and more courteous she becomes, and the more unbearable the calm reads. She will not break, will not refuse, will not end it. **This is the shock:** somewhere on the other end of a working wire, a person is listening to them drown and choosing procedure.
- **NPC State Change (players):** The committee now *knows in the gut* the phone will never save them — not because the line is down, but because the woman on it won't. Whatever hope they'd hung on "calling out" is gone.

### Say her name — the wild guess
- **Requires:** The table has learned [Hania Barnaś](../characters/jagna.md) was real and *vanished* — [barnas-had-a-daughter](../clues/clues.md#barnas-had-a-daughter) and [jagna-fled-the-lynch](../clues/clues.md#jagna-fled-the-lynch). Without that, there is no name to reach for.
- **Cost:** Free — it's a blurted intuition, not a considered move. No evidence, pure gut: the vanished girl and this cold voice colliding.
- **Outcome:** A player says it straight down the wire — *"...Hania?"* — or hits the one nerve only she would flinch at (her father, the well, *"Edek's still alive"*). Run **the gasp** (see her [GM note](../characters/secondary-characters.md#operator-the-telephone-exchange-operator)): a small caught breath escapes her before she can stop it. Then — *same breath* — the cage slams shut, flat again, *"...Number, please,"* and she **keeps going.** She does not hang up. She reseals into the script and carries on processing them, exactly as before, as if the crack never happened.
  - That's the horror of it: the mask slipped for half a second, and *she's still there.* Still on the line. Still calm. Still helping with nothing.
  - **Discipline (from her GM note):** involuntary then instantly sealed; **unrepeatable** (say the name again and there's only flat procedure); **deniable** (gasp, or static catching on a bad line?); **one trigger only** — the single unbearable thing, never every heavy topic. Never confirm it. They walk away certain and empty-handed.
- **Dependency:** this beat only detonates for a table that dug into '54. For players who never did, there is no name to guess and the call plays as pure bureaucratic cold — still eerie, but the payoff belongs to those who earned it.

## If Missed

If the players never call out — or give up before the guess — the event still resolves: the flood eventually takes the line itself. A pole comes down somewhere between the valley and the town, and the connection dies on its own.

**She never ends it.** She would have stayed on that line, courteous and useless, until the water reached the wires. To the GM, that's the whole point: the valley's one voice to the outside world was hers all along, and she held it open just to make them feel it close. The phone was never rescue — the powiat's *"we'll send someone when conditions allow"* (see [the flood](the-flood.md)) is the proof; bureaucracy doesn't rescue. She only put a face on a valley that was already drowning.
