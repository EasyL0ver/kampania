## Character

Not a farmer and never has been — he's a driver, on the road hauling for the PGR and whoever else needs a lorry, home at odd hours. He holds the father's deed to the house and the border strip his sister has farmed for years. He isn't cruel and he isn't scheming: the paper is his, the flood means real money, and he wants his share. Reasonable, unanswerable, and — without meaning to be — the wall the committee can't climb. He was away on the road when the father died, which is why it was his sister at the bedside, not him — and why the old man's dying words went to her, not him.

## Appearance

- **Clothes:** Everyday village clothes, but not a worker's — a jacket that's seen better days over a clean-ish shirt, boots that don't spend their days in a field
- **Hair & face:** Short practical haircut, clean-shaven or a day's stubble; a soft, unremarkable, tired face — not a man who labours outdoors
- **Carriage:** Stands with his weight back, hands in pockets — unhurried, a man who knows he doesn't need to win the argument because the paper already has

Speaks plainly and without heat, the way a man does when he knows the rules are already on his side. No raised voice, no pleading — he doesn't have to. Reasonable to the point that it's hard to hate him, which is exactly what makes ruling against him feel like the crime.
