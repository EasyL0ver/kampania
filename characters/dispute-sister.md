## Character

She married into a farming family and has worked the border strip beside her father's old house for years — real labour, season after season, while her brother was off driving his lorry and never touched it. Now the flood puts money on the dirt and he waves their dead father's deed. She has no paper, only her hands and one thing her father told her as he died. She doesn't understand what that thing is — but she'll say it out loud to anyone who'll take her side, because it's the only weapon she has.

- **Clothes:** Work clothes — a headscarf, a heavy skirt, a man's jacket over it, boots caked from the field; hands red and cracked
- **Hair & face:** Weathered younger than her years; hair pulled back hard; a set jaw that goes unsteady when the strip comes up
- **Carriage:** Strong, quick, a worker's economy of movement — but she folds her arms tight and won't quite look at you when she talks about her father's last days

Blunt and tired, not tearful — a working woman stating her case, not begging. She gets sharp and fast when the deed is mentioned, and oddly quiet, almost spooked, when she repeats what her father said at the end — like she's handling something she doesn't want to hold.
