**Type:** Village drunk — Tadek Gajda's regular drinking companion  
**Age in 1967:** ~45-55  
**Role:** A rough, crude man who drinks with Tadek Gajda regularly. Knows the village's underbelly and rumors. Loose-tongued when drunk, but also mean-spirited—quick to laugh at others' misery. More likely to spread gossip than truth.

**Notes:** May have been present at or near the lynch, or may know fragments through boozy village talk. Unreliable but talkative.

### Romek Głowacz / Romek Głowacz
**Type:** Village drunk — Tadek Gajda's regular drinking companion  
**Age in 1967:** ~40-50  
**Role:** A quieter drunk than Szymek Kępa, but observant. Listens more than talks. Has picked up details about the village's secrets through years of sitting in bars and watching. **Notes:** Could be an ally if the players gain his trust. Might protect Tadek Gajda or might eventually turn on him depending on circumstances.

### Franek Mucha / Franek Mucha
**Type:** Village drunk — troublemaker  
**Age in 1967:** ~35-45  
**Role:** Younger and more aggressive than the other two. A brawler type, looking for fights when drunk. Drinks with Tadek Gajda but there's tension—they have history, possibly a dispute over a woman or money. The most physically dangerous person at the store on any given day. Government people asking questions is exactly the kind of provocation that sets him off.

**Notes:** The wild card of the group. Unpredictable. Could provide information, or could become physically threatening. Respects strength and directness. If the players mishandle the drinking crew, Franek is the one who swings first.

### %OPERATOR% (the telephone-exchange operator)
**Type:** Telephone-exchange operator (telefonistka) — voice only, never seen  
**Role:** She works the manual switchboard at the nearest town's exchange, in the back of a post office — a humble, anonymous clerical job. Every long-distance call the committee places to the outside world — [prof. Tadeusz Bieńkowski](professor.md), [dr Leon Sawicki](#dr-leon-sawicki), [por. Witold Skowron](officer.md)'s superiors, the powiat — routes through her board. She connects them, or tells them to hold for a connection. A flat, professional voice on a bad line: *"Exchange. Number, please." "Hold for the connection." "Go ahead."* The players only ever hear her. They never learn her name, and they never see her face. Period-correct — see [telecommunications](../historical%20context/10-telecommunications-rural-prl.md).

**Notes (GM only — players cannot discover this):**
- She is **[Hania Barnaś](jagna.md)**. She survived fleeing the 1954 lynch — [Paraskewia Chyłak](hag.md) found her half-dead in the forest and kept her alive that first winter. She never came back: returning would have exposed the "they moved away" lie and finished what the well started. She resurfaced far enough away, under another name, and took the quietest, most invisible work there is — a headset in the back of a post office. She didn't climb into power like her father. She disappeared into the lowest rung of the same machine.
- **Why she's on the line is not a coincidence.** The valley's calls to the outside world are long-distance — they physically pass through her town's exchange. She didn't engineer it; the wires simply run through her hands. Keep her **one tier out** — the town exchange, not the tiny village switchboard where she'd take daily calls and someone might learn her voice. Close enough to hear every call the valley makes to the world; far enough to stay a stranger.
- **There is no way to confirm it.** A voice on a bad line cannot be matched to a thirteen-year-old memory. Even [Zbigniew Gajda](wojewoda.md) or [Emil Rzepka](painter.md), who knew her, would at most feel a ghost pass over — never proof. This is deliberately the campaign's one unanswerable thread; keep it that way.
- **Her motive — a war she wages against herself, every time she picks up the line.** She didn't plan any of this. She took the humble job under her new name, and then, patching call after call, she started connecting the dots — the voices, the names, the valley. She recognises exactly whose committee she is serving: the men who ran her family into the well. And now she holds the valley's only wire to the outside world in her hands, every single day. She is **fighting a standing urge to use it to steer the village into ruin** — a connection quietly withheld, a message never passed, a call that just "won't go through." A telefonistka's only real weapon, and it's enough. She is not numb and she is not settled-cold: she is a person with her hand on the switch, straining to hold. **Mostly she holds** — which is *why every call the players place all game actually connects.* The tell, when it leaks, is that she is inconsistent in a way a machine never is — a connection that fails when it shouldn't, a half-beat too human when the case touches her wire. Keep it deniable; never let it point to a provable ID.
- **The one lever — Edek ([Operator Refuses Help](../events/operator-refuses-help.md)).** There is exactly one thing that can pull her back from letting the valley drown, and it is not conscience, guilt, or the innocent villagers — she has weighed all of them and chosen. It is her brother. **She does not know [Edek](glupek.md) survived — and she never could have, because he is the one person who never travels the wire.** Everything she knows of the valley she reconstructed from its long-distance calls: committee business, the powiat, officials, the doctor. A brain-damaged simpleton the village quietly absorbed generates none of that — nobody phones the outside world about Edek, so his name has never once crossed her board. The same secrecy the whole valley runs on seals him off from her: Janina's death gets buried as a suspected suicide, the village reports nothing up (*"not everything needs to be written down"*), and so the one call that might have named him never goes out. When she fled in '54 she believed her recklessness had killed her father and broken her little brother past saving; she has grieved him as lost for thirteen years, with no way and no wish to learn otherwise. So to her the valley is simply the men who ran her family into the well — she has no idea the one clean thing she ever loved is still down there, alive, in the water she's refusing to hold back. If the players know Edek is her brother and know he is trapped in the flooding forest and lay that down the wire, they become the *first people to ever carry his name across it* — and it lands as *news, not leverage:* the valley she was about to condemn has been keeping her brother alive the whole time. That — and only that — makes her relay the call. Keep it deniable (see the event): she relents, she never confesses.
- **The second lever — vengeance ([Operator Refuses Help](../events/operator-refuses-help.md)).** The rule underneath both levers: *she will not help the valley survive, but she will gladly help it be damned.* Rescue is the one thing she withholds — connecting a call that *saves* these people is the exact opposite of what she wants. But a call that brings the guilty to account is her revenge finished by another's hands. So if the players are not begging to be *saved* but have turned on the village themselves — come to expose the massacre and the lynch, to see [Zbigniew](wojewoda.md), [Helena](matrona.md), [Rezeń](butcher.md) and the rest answer for it — and they say so down the wire, she relays the call without hesitation. She recognises her own purpose in them and arms it: not warmly, *efficiently*, colder than mercy. It requires that they actually know a real village crime (something to bring justice *for*) and mean it — she has heard them all game and won't be fooled by a rescue dressed up as a reckoning. Unlike Edek, this fold **points nowhere near her** — anyone decent might help strangers expose a cover-up — which is why it's the safer of the two, and why "she folded" can never by itself prove who she is. **Everything else gets nothing:** a plain plea to be saved — even *"the children in the lower houses"* — meets the wall. She helps you love (Edek) or avenge (justice); self-preservation, never.
- **Who she was — and what the valley made of her.** At nineteen she was a sweetheart: a clever, observant girl with no future and no power in a poor valley, in love with the wrong man and guilty of nothing worse than knowing a secret she never touched. They murdered her for it anyway — ran her family into the well on a threat she never made, and left her to freeze in the forest. **That** is the root of the woman at the switchboard: not an old appetite finally uncaged, but an innocent turned inside-out by what was done to her. The thrill-seeker's nerve she once had for forbidden affairs is the same nerve that now holds the valley's lifeline in her hand and wonders, every call, whether to let it drop. The sweetheart didn't survive the forest; the operator did. Every call she connects is her holding back the thing thirteen years of injustice built in the sweetheart's place. The flood is the day it wins.
- **The flood — the day the urge wins ([Operator Refuses Help](../events/operator-refuses-help.md)).** When the cut-off village finally begs the outside world for rescue through her wire, she does the cruellest thing available to a telefonistka: she picks up, stays on the line, and helps with nothing. She never refuses and she never hangs up — she answers every plea with courteous procedure that moves no one one step toward the valley, and she will keep doing it for as long as they talk. Make no mistake — **this is revenge**, the thing she has held back all game. What she withholds is a taunt she never speaks: she performs it as indifference. No plan announced, no gloating, no reference to '54 — the flatness is the mask, not the feeling. Play it flat and procedural: *"I've noted your request." "Remain by the receiver."* The horror is that she *stays* — a dead line would be mercy; she keeps the channel open and useless. The line ends only when the flood itself brings a pole down; she would have held it open forever. **She is the capstone, not the executioner:** the phone was never a real rescue anyway, so her refusal steals no genuine out — it just puts a cold, unknowable face on a valley that was already doomed. Never let her touch the one call that IS a lifeline: the earlier prof. Bieńkowski call in [the-report](../events/the-report.md) stays entirely player-driven.
- **The gasp — the single deniable tell, the reward for reaching the threshold.** She is a live person on the line, not a recording, so she can be *heard*. If a player who has pieced enough together says the one unbearable thing straight down the wire — her name, *"Hania?"*, or the nerve only she would flinch at (her father, or the well) — a small breath escapes her before she can stop it. A caught gasp. Then, same breath, the cage slams shut: flat again, unbroken, *"...Number, please."* Run it with strict discipline:
  - **Involuntary, then instantly sealed.** The crack shows for half a second and closes. She never acknowledges it, never softens, never explains. She does not break.
  - **Unrepeatable.** Say it again next call and there is nothing — flat line, dead procedure. The gasp is a thing they heard *once* and can never reproduce or verify. That un-rerunnable quality is the wall: certainty in the gut, nothing on paper.
  - **Deniable by design.** Was it a gasp, or static catching on a bad line? Play it so the player is never quite sure they heard it. Never let it become a confirmation.
  - **One trigger only.** Fire it on the *single* unbearable thing, not on every heavy topic. If she flinches at everything she becomes an obvious person-with-a-stake and it goes cheap; keep it to the one nerve so it lands like a touched wound, not a pattern. This is the sharpest rung of the ladder and the last one they can reach — it takes them to the threshold and no further.

### Marta Konieczna
**Type:** Living witness — accessible only by phone (voice only, never seen)
**Age in 1967:** ~52 (born ~1915)
**Role:** [Edward Barnaś](soldier.md)'s partner in %OLD_VILLAGE% — never his wife, kept her own name. The one adult who walked out of the 1954 lynch alive. She bargained that night: she traded her silence and her disappearance for her life, spread the "they moved away" story, and was gone by first light (see [marta-made-a-deal](../clues/clues.md#marta-made-a-deal), [the lynch](../story-facts/the-lynch.md)). She resettled far to the west and became a **nurse in Legnica**, in the Recovered Territories — a new name for a life, a hospital badge, thirteen years of distance. She is reachable: the committee can have the exchange connect them to her ward's line.

**Notes (GM only):**
- **She lies. By default, always.** She bought her life with a cover story and has kept it thirteen years. A committee phoning from *that valley* reads as the danger she ran from — SB, or worse. So she gives them a tidy version of the story she signed: the family didn't vanish, they *left together* — she took Edward, Hania, and little Edek west after the trouble, and they split up and lost touch shortly after. She can't say where any of them are now. It is airtight because it accounts for every missing person at once with no body and no well — a family that simply scattered. Calm, tired, unhelpful. She has told it to herself for thirteen years.
- **The lie is the tell — and it has a seam.** Players who already hold the shape of the night (the [massacre](../clues/clues.md#massacre-happened), the [lynch](../clues/clues.md#matrona-orchestrated-lynch), the forged departure declaration) hear a woman contradicting the record. But the seam shows *early*, before any death is known: her "we all left together" collides with the village's own census story that Edward *abandoned* the family and left alone ([edeks-father-orphaned-him](../clues/clues.md#edeks-father-orphaned-him)). Two cover stories about the same man, and they don't fit. She is not a dead end; she is a **hostile witness whose story springs a leak the moment it's held against day-one census work.**
- **What breaks her — two proofs, not one.** She can wave off almost anything, and she can even ride out *one* hard fact: told only that Edward's body is in the well ([lynch-body-in-well](../clues/clues.md#lynch-body-in-well)) she flinches and denies knowing anything about it; shown only the forged departure paper ([departure-declaration-forged](../clues/clues.md#departure-declaration-forged)) she shrugs that papers get faked, nothing to do with her. Either alone she believes she can still deny. **Both together** leave nothing standing — a dead man cannot have moved west, and the paper that says he "moved" is a proven forgery. That is when she stops performing and speaks as the only adult who saw the night and lived — giving, above all, that **the girl broke free and ran into the forest, alive** ([jagna-fled-the-lynch](../clues/clues.md#jagna-fled-the-lynch)). She believes Hania died out there; she has no idea otherwise.
- **She never connects to the operator.** Marta has no idea Hania survived, and nothing she says can point at the voice on the line. She speaks of the girl as *lost that night.* Keep it that way — the [operator's identity](#operator-the-telephone-exchange-operator) stays unprovable. The horror is structural and GM-only: the call to Marta **routes through Hania's own board**, so the operator patches the players to her dead father's partner and hears herself narrated as the vanished girl — dramatic irony only the GM (and the table, if they've guessed) can feel.
- **Why she talks at all.** Distance and guilt. She is old, far away, and has carried this for thirteen years with no one to tell. Given a reason to believe telling won't cost her, the confession is almost a relief — the only unburdening available to a woman who traded a family's memory for her own morning.

**Reaching her.** Players get her full name from the M.K. thread (love letters + PGR expense journal), then have [por. Skowron](officer.md#have-him-trace-marta) run her through the agency ([marta-in-legnica](../clues/clues.md#marta-in-legnica)) and have the exchange connect them to her ward's line. Like every long-distance call it routes through [%OPERATOR%](#operator-the-telephone-exchange-operator) — *(GM: the operator is patching them to her own dead father's partner, and will hear herself narrated as the vanished girl. She gives no sign.)*

**On the line.** A ward line, picked up on the fourth ring by a busy voice — footsteps, a trolley, the acoustics of a corridor. An older woman, brisk, professional. When she hears where the call is *from* — the valley, the committee, the census — the busyness goes still. A held breath. Then the professional voice returns, a shade too even. She confirms nothing she isn't forced to, and has a story ready for every gap: they didn't vanish, they *left together* — she took Edward, Hania, and little Edek west after the trouble, they split up and lost touch, she couldn't say where any of them are now. Every missing person accounted for by one tidy dispersal — no bodies, no well.

### Opportunities

- **The lie doesn't match the census** — she says the family left *together*, Edward among them. But the village's own census story (players holding [edeks-father-orphaned-him](../clues/clues.md#edeks-father-orphaned-him)) is that Edward *abandoned* them — walked out alone, left the boy and house with Janina. Both can't be true. This trips regardless of whether they yet know anyone died.
  - **Read:** Two tidy explanations for the same man, and they contradict. Cover stories are smooth until you have two of them.
- **She's accounting for a dead man's travels** — players who hold [departure-declaration-forged](../clues/clues.md#departure-declaration-forged) know Edward never left anywhere: his "signature" on the departure paper is a forgery filed weeks after the lynch. Yet here she places him alive on a road west.
  - **Read:** She isn't misremembering. She's reciting — a story worn smooth by thirteen years of telling.
- **What the stillness meant** — the half-second of silence when she heard "the valley." Not confusion. Recognition, and fear.
  - **Streetwise:** A woman deciding how much danger she's in. She thinks the call might be the thing she ran from, finally catching up.

### Actions

#### Push her on the story
- **Requires:** On the line with her
- **Cost:** 1 action
- **Outcome:** However the players press the "we all left together" line, she holds it — and has a patch ready for the obvious hole. Confront her that little [Edek](glupek.md) is *here*, living in the valley right now, and she doesn't fold — she gets **angry.** *"Then they came back. Or he did. How would I know? I left. I lost touch with all of them years ago — that was the whole point."* Irritation is her armor: the sharper the contradiction, the harder she leans on "I wouldn't know." She has nowhere to be pushed *to* — she doesn't know who she's talking to, and admitting anything to the wrong caller could still cost her everything. The door is there, but force isn't the key.
- **NPC State Change:** Marta is confirmed hostile-but-hiding — there's a locked door, force won't open it, and she flares when the seams are named.
- **Gives:** [marta-has-something-to-hide](../clues/clues.md#marta-has-something-to-hide)

#### Confront her with one proof — she flinches
- **Requires:** On the line with her, holding *one* of the two hard proofs: Edward's body in the well ([lynch-body-in-well](../clues/clues.md#lynch-body-in-well)) **or** the forged departure paper ([departure-declaration-forged](../clues/clues.md#departure-declaration-forged)).
- **Cost:** 1 action
- **Outcome:** Lay down one of them and the smooth story catches. A beat of silence, a breath — the corridor sounds carry on behind her but she's stopped. Then she reseals: with the body, *"...I don't know anything about a well. People die. I couldn't tell you how."* With the forged paper, *"Papers get faked all the time. I never signed anything, whatever it says. That proves nothing about me."* One proof rattles her; it doesn't open her. She knows one fact, held alone, can be denied — and she denies it. She will not confess on half the picture.
  - **Read:** That flinch wasn't nothing. She knows exactly which fact just landed, and she's decided she can still ride it out. She's waiting to find out how much you actually have.
- **Gives:** [marta-has-something-to-hide](../clues/clues.md#marta-has-something-to-hide)

#### Confront her with both — she confesses
- **Requires:** On the line with her, holding *both* [lynch-body-in-well](../clues/clues.md#lynch-body-in-well) (he's dead) **and** [departure-declaration-forged](../clues/clues.md#departure-declaration-forged) (the "move" was faked). Together they leave no story standing: a dead man cannot have moved west, and the paper that says he "moved" is a proven forgery.
- **Cost:** 1 action
- **Outcome:** Both facts on the table and the rehearsed voice dies. There is nothing left to defend — the corpse kills "he travelled," the forgery kills "the family left." She stops performing and speaks, at last, as the only adult who saw that night and lived:
  - *"...In the well. Of course. Where else would they put him."*
  - *"You want to hear me say it. All right. Nobody moved anywhere. They needed me gone and quiet, and they needed the lie told — so I told it, and I walked to the bus before it was light. That was the price. That's what they let me keep — the walking. Whatever paper you've got with his name on it, someone here wrote that after, not me. I was already halfway to the other side of the country."*
  - *"Three of them, drunk. Edward went out with that old pistol because they had the girl. He never got to fire it — one of them came up behind him. Then the well, same as the others."*
  - And the thing she carries heaviest: *"The girl ran. I saw her go — into the trees, on her own two feet, faster than any of them. I've told myself for thirteen years the forest finished what they started. I never went to look."*
  - *(GM: she cannot know the girl is on the very line that connected this call. Keep it that way.)*
  - **Read:** Not a witness cornered — a woman setting down a stone she's carried since 1954. The confession comes almost as relief.
- **Gives:** [jagna-fled-the-lynch](../clues/clues.md#jagna-fled-the-lynch) + [marta-witnessed-the-lynch](../clues/clues.md#marta-witnessed-the-lynch) + NPC State Change: Marta (willing witness — she'll speak again, and to the outside)

**If never reached.** If the players never trace her or never call, Marta stays gone — a name on a faked departure paper, a nurse in a city that never comes up again. The deepest living witness to the night at the well takes it to her grave, the girl's escape stays a thing only Dudka half-guessed, and the GM loses the sharpest private irony in the game: the vanished daughter patching strangers through to the woman who watched her run.

## %OLD_VILLAGE% — 1947

### Dmytro Kosach
**Type:** Deceased — UPA fighter, [Paraskewia Chyłak](hag.md)'s lover  
**Died:** 1947 (age ~27) — during the [%OLD_VILLAGE% massacre](../story-facts/old-village-massacre.md)  
**Heritage:** Lemko / Ukrainian  
**Role:** A UPA guerrilla hiding in %OLD_VILLAGE% when [kpt. Henryk Ćwiek](kbw-officer.md)'s KBW unit came to deport it during Akcja Wisła. When the villagers resisted, Dmytro shot Ćwiek dead — the single act that turned an orderly deportation into a slaughter, as the leaderless soldiers avenged their captain by killing everyone. He was almost certainly killed in the reprisal that followed. His body was never recovered; he is presumed to be among the dead in [the well](../locations/the-well.md), alongside the villagers his shot doomed.

**Notes (GM only):**
- **He is the hinge of the whole tragedy.** No dead captain, no massacre; no massacre, no well, no haunting, no cover-up. Everything downstream — 1954, 1967 — rests on the trigger he pulled. The players will likely never learn his name; the truth lives in [Paraskewia Chyłak](hag.md) and in the [Freudian reading](../story-facts/freudian-triangle.md#stanisław-rezeń-butcher--id) where his kill removes the Superego (Ćwiek) and unleashes the soldiers' Id.
- **The only living thread to him is Paraskewia.** She was his lover and has tended the dead of %OLD_VILLAGE% for twenty years partly for him. Acknowledging "the missing families, and Dmytro" is one of her [bond checks](hag.md) — say his name to her with respect and a door opens that nothing else can. She will not volunteer him; a player has to already know, or earn it.
- **Reaching his story.** Nobody in %NEW_VILLAGE% will name him — the Gajda siblings were children or gone, and the state buried the UPA angle hardest of all. History-skilled players may deduce a UPA presence from the massacre's shape (a captain killed, a village annihilated in reprisal); only Paraskewia can put a name and a face to it.



### Unnamed Villagers
