## Tadek Gajda's Drinking Circle

### Szymek Kępa / Szymek Kępa
**Type:** Village drunk — Tadek Gajda's regular drinking companion  
**Age in 1967:** ~45-55  
**Role:** A rough, crude man who drinks with Tadek Gajda regularly. Knows the village's underbelly and rumors. Loose-tongued when drunk, but also mean-spirited—quick to laugh at others' misery. More likely to spread gossip than truth.

**Notes:** May have been present at or near the lynch, or may know fragments through boozy village talk. Unreliable but talkative.

### Romek Głowacz / Romek Głowacz
**Type:** Village drunk — Tadek Gajda's regular drinking companion  
**Age in 1967:** ~40-50  
**Role:** A quieter drunk than Szymek Kępa, but observant. Listens more than talks. Has picked up details about the village's secrets through years of sitting in bars and watching. Less likely to volunteer information, but can be drawn out carefully. Knows Tadek Gajda better than most—they have a deeper, more genuine friendship underneath the drinking.

**Notes:** Could be an ally if the players gain his trust. Might protect Tadek Gajda or might eventually turn on him depending on circumstances.

### Franek Mucha / Franek Mucha
**Type:** Village drunk — troublemaker  
**Age in 1967:** ~35-45  
**Role:** Younger and more aggressive than the other two. A brawler type, looking for fights when drunk. Drinks with Tadek Gajda but there's tension—they have history, possibly a dispute over a woman or money. The most physically dangerous person at the store on any given day. Government people asking questions is exactly the kind of provocation that sets him off.

**Notes:** The wild card of the group. Unpredictable. Could provide information, or could become physically threatening. Respects strength and directness. If the players mishandle the drinking crew, Franek is the one who swings first.

## Helena Rzepka and Emil Rzepka's Household

### Ewa Rzepka / Ewa Rzepka
**Type:** Young woman — Helena Rzepka and Emil Rzepka's eldest child  
**Age in 1967:** ~13-15  
**Role:** Worried about her father—she sees his fragility and broken state. This concern for Emil Rzepka is her defining trait. Could be sympathetic to players or protective of her family depending on the situation. A potential crack in Helena Rzepka's control.

**Notes:** If players can reach Ewa Rzepka, she might reveal things about the household dynamic or her mother's cruelty.

### Krystian Rzepka
**Type:** Child — Helena Rzepka and Emil Rzepka's youngest, altar boy  
**Age in 1967:** ~8-9  
**Role:** Serves at the church assisting ks. Władysław Pająk. Sees and hears confessional traffic, notices who comes and goes. Helena Rzepka placed him in this role to maintain the family's pious reputation. Innocent but observant. First loyalty is to Helena Rzepka.

**Notes:** May witness Edek Barnaś hiding in the church. His cooperation with players depends on whether they can separate him from his mother's influence.

## Helena Rzepka's Store

### Halina Zając / Halina Zając
**Type:** Store worker — Helena Rzepka's employee  
**Age in 1967:** ~35-45  
**Role:** Works in Helena Rzepka's small general store. Deals with customers, manages stock, handles money. Sees everyone who comes through—knows the village's patterns and gossip. Resentful of working for Helena Rzepka and struggles with the drinking crew (Szymek Kępa, Romek Głowacz, Franek Mucha) who hang around and cause trouble in the store.

**Notes:** A gatekeeper to the store with a mean streak. Could be sympathetic to players if they take her side against the drunks, or hostile if they seem to align with her antagonists. Her resentment makes her more likely to complain about Helena Rzepka than to protect her secrets.

### %OPERATOR% (the telephone-exchange operator)
**Type:** Telephone-exchange operator (telefonistka) — voice only, never seen  
**Role:** She works the manual switchboard at the nearest town's exchange, in the back of a post office — a humble, anonymous clerical job. Every long-distance call the committee places to the outside world — [prof. Tadeusz Bieńkowski](professor.md), [dr Leon Sawicki](#dr-leon-sawicki), [por. Witold Skowron](officer.md)'s superiors, the powiat — routes through her board. She connects them, or tells them to hold for a connection. A flat, professional voice on a bad line: *"Exchange. Number, please." "Hold for the connection." "Go ahead."* The players only ever hear her. They never learn her name, and they never see her face. Period-correct — see [telecommunications](../historical%20context/10-telecommunications-rural-prl.md).

**Notes (GM only — players cannot discover this):**
- She is **[Hania Barnaś](jagna.md)**. She survived fleeing the 1954 lynch — [Paraskewia Chyłak](hag.md) found her half-dead in the forest and kept her alive that first winter. She never came back: returning would have exposed the "they moved away" lie and finished what the well started. She resurfaced far enough away, under another name, and took the quietest, most invisible work there is — a headset in the back of a post office. She didn't climb into power like her father. She disappeared into the lowest rung of the same machine.
- **Why she's on the line is not a coincidence.** The valley's calls to the outside world are long-distance — they physically pass through her town's exchange. She didn't engineer it; the wires simply run through her hands. Keep her **one tier out** — the town exchange, not the tiny village switchboard where she'd take daily calls and someone might learn her voice. Close enough to hear every call the valley makes to the world; far enough to stay a stranger.
- **There is no way to confirm it.** A voice on a bad line cannot be matched to a nineteen-year-old's hidden photograph. Even [Zbigniew Gajda](wojewoda.md) or [Emil Rzepka](painter.md), who knew her, would at most feel a ghost pass over — never proof. This is deliberately the campaign's one unanswerable thread; keep it that way.
- **Her motive — a war she wages against herself, every time she picks up the line.** She didn't plan any of this. She took the humble job under her new name, and then, patching call after call, she started connecting the dots — the voices, the names, the valley. She recognises exactly whose committee she is serving: the men who ran her family into the well. And now she holds the valley's only wire to the outside world in her hands, every single day. She is **fighting a standing urge to use it to steer the village into ruin** — a connection quietly withheld, a message never passed, a call that just "won't go through." A telefonistka's only real weapon, and it's enough. She is not numb and she is not settled-cold: she is a person with her hand on the switch, straining to hold. **Mostly she holds** — which is *why every call the players place all game actually connects.* The tell, when it leaks, is that she is inconsistent in a way a machine never is — a connection that fails when it shouldn't, a half-beat too human when the case touches her wire. Keep it deniable; never let it point to a provable ID.
- **Who she was — the other half of the war.** At nineteen she was a thrill-seeker: a clever girl with no future and no power in a poor valley, who made her own stakes out of forbidden affairs and other people's secrets. That appetite is what got her family killed — she blackmailed the Gajdas with a secret she wasn't afraid to wield (see [the lynch](../story-facts/the-lynch.md) and [her file](jagna.md)). So the urge at the switchboard isn't only revenge; it's the **same old hunger** she once fed and that cost her everything. She caged it in the most boring, invisible job on earth on purpose. Every call she connects is her keeping the cage shut. The flood is the day it opens.
- **The flood — the day the urge wins ([Operator Refuses Help](../events/operator-refuses-help.md)).** When the cut-off village finally begs the outside world for rescue through her wire, she does the cruellest thing available to a telefonistka: she picks up, stays on the line, and helps with nothing. She never refuses and she never hangs up — she answers every plea with courteous procedure that moves no one one step toward the valley, and she will keep doing it for as long as they talk. Make no mistake — **this is revenge**, the thing she has held back all game. What she withholds is a taunt she never speaks: she performs it as indifference. No plan announced, no gloating, no reference to '54 — the flatness is the mask, not the feeling. Play it flat and procedural: *"I've noted your request." "Remain by the receiver."* The horror is that she *stays* — a dead line would be mercy; she keeps the channel open and useless. The line ends only when the flood itself brings a pole down; she would have held it open forever. **She is the capstone, not the executioner:** the phone was never a real rescue anyway, so her refusal steals no genuine out — it just puts a cold, unknowable face on a valley that was already doomed. Never let her touch the one call that IS a lifeline: the earlier prof. Bieńkowski call in [the-report](../events/the-report.md) stays entirely player-driven.
- **The gasp — the single deniable tell, the reward for reaching the threshold.** She is a live person on the line, not a recording, so she can be *heard*. If a player who has pieced enough together says the one unbearable thing straight down the wire — her name, *"Hania?"*, or the nerve only she would flinch at (her father, the well, *"Edek's still alive"*) — a small breath escapes her before she can stop it. A caught gasp. Then, same breath, the cage slams shut: flat again, unbroken, *"...Number, please."* Run it with strict discipline:
  - **Involuntary, then instantly sealed.** The crack shows for half a second and closes. She never acknowledges it, never softens, never explains. She does not break.
  - **Unrepeatable.** Say it again next call and there is nothing — flat line, dead procedure. The gasp is a thing they heard *once* and can never reproduce or verify. That un-rerunnable quality is the wall: certainty in the gut, nothing on paper.
  - **Deniable by design.** Was it a gasp, or static catching on a bad line? Play it so the player is never quite sure they heard it. Never let it become a confirmation.
  - **One trigger only.** Fire it on the *single* unbearable thing, not on every heavy topic. If she flinches at everything she becomes an obvious person-with-a-stake and it goes cheap; keep it to the one nerve so it lands like a touched wound, not a pattern. This is the sharpest rung of the ladder and the last one they can reach — it takes them to the threshold and no further.

## Notes on Secondary Characters

- **Names in %PLACEHOLDER%:** Assign real Polish names as needed.
- **Flexibility:** Secondary characters should be present enough to feel like a real village, but vague enough that they can be adjusted based on player interaction.
- **Utility:** Each should serve a specific narrative purpose—gossip, witness, vulnerability, comic relief, moral test, etc.
- **Expandability:** If a secondary character becomes important to the story, promote them to a dedicated character file.
