%NEW_VILLAGE% on the Solina Shore — Spill-Routed Terrain

The only objects are points. A feature is a bundle of points and the relations between them, never a drawn shape: a ridge is points held high, a basin a low point, the gap a low notch. The ground is then modelled until it satisfies the story: the valley is raised until it is a sealed catchment whose only low way out is the gap. Flooding is not a painted water level. Water is routed: the lake rises in a basin until it tops its lowest sill, then spills to the next basin (fill-to-spill, priority-flood). So the gap plug decides where the water goes, and the same terrain floods the village when the gap is shut and %BIG-BASIN% when it is open. Change the seed for another valley.

Gap:

North is up. The valley has three candidate outlets: the ridge water-gap (to %BIG-BASIN%), the streambed col (to the next valley), the PGR ditch. When the gap is plugged, the lowest outlet left is the col, whose sill stands above the houses, so the rising lake tops the village before it can spill. Open the gap and the lake spills below house level, the village stays dry, and the water fills %BIG-BASIN% instead.
