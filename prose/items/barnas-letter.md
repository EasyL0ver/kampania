## Source

The attic of [Ciotka's House](../locations/ciotkas-house.md) — Edward Barnaś's old house

## Carried

Evidence. Mail that reached a dead man a year after the village recorded him gone.

## Description

A sealed envelope gone soft and yellow with damp and years, never opened. It is addressed to Edward Barnaś at the village in a heavy, barrack-trained hand, and the postmark is dated 1955. Inside is a single folded sheet from an old service comrade.

## Content

> Edward,
>
> Too long since the mountains. I still think on that winter up there, the cold that got into the rifles, the men who are not around now to grumble about it. You were the steady one. I have not forgotten.
>
> They have me on the pots these days, feeding a barracks full of boys who never knew a hard winter. And you a farmer now, of all things, pulling turnips while I burn soup. Who would have thought it of the two of us.
>
> Things are turning back here. People ask questions they would not have dared a few years ago. Old business. Old names. I sleep badly for it. I wanted to say it to you first, before it reaches you another way.
>
> Say a word to Rezeń for me, if the old devil is still up there near you.
>
> Keep well, and tell me the land turned out good.
>
> Bronek

*Nothing in the words names a uniform. A soldier's or clerk's eye, though, reads the sender's rank and unit markings on the sheet as KBW, the internal security corps.*
