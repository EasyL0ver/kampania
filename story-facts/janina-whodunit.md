# The Death of Janina Gajda

**Type:** Core mystery, present-tense case

**Status:** Locked design. The truth and the suspect structure are final. Scene and clue wiring is the remaining work (see Follow-Up).

## The Logline

Everybody broke her, nobody killed her. [Janina Gajda](../characters/ciotka.md) took her own life under the weight of the buried 1954 crime and the net closing around it. Three people look guilty. All three are innocent of murder. The village, desperate to keep 1954 buried, scapegoats the one person it always scapegoats: an innocent Barnaś.

## The Truth (GM-Only)

Janina is the moral heart and the living witness of 1954. She saved [Edek](../characters/glupek.md) from [Rezeń](../characters/butcher.md) and has guarded that secret and that boy for thirteen years. By the time the committee arrives, the net is closing: the census is prying at the family, [Marek "Junior" Gajda](../characters/junior.md) has been pressing her for his mother's private investigation, and the flood is coming to drown the well and wash away the evidence. She decides the secret cannot hold.

She tells Edek the truth: these people killed his father, and he is the living proof. Edek, brain-damaged and unable to hold it, panics. He grabs or shoves her, leaves a mark, and flees into the night. Alone, injured, having finally spoken the truth and seeing no way through that does not destroy the boy or damn her own blood, Janina takes her own life with her own prescribed Luminal.

No one murdered her. The moral killers are 1954 and everyone's prying, the players' included. The rest was her own hand.

## Why This Case Works

- **Present-tense and playable.** One victim, one night, a finite suspect pool all still in the village, unfolding during play. Unlike 1947 and 1954 (collective, past-tense, no agency), this is a case the committee can actually investigate with stakes.
- **It re-runs 1954 in real time.** The village closes on an innocent Barnaś to avoid the truth, exactly as it did to [Edward Barnaś](../characters/soldier.md). This time the players are present and can stop the repeat.
- **It indicts investigation itself.** The players are outside investigators prying at a buried crime. Junior is their mirror. Solving the case means recognizing that their kind of pressure is part of what broke her.

## The Scapegoat: Edek

Edek is who everyone blames and hunts. He is huge, feared, brain-damaged; he fled the scene; there is a mark on the body; his footprints run to the treeline. Of course the village decides the idiot killed his aunt. Rezeń and [Dudka](../characters/neighbour.md) go after him. The players' real job is not to catch a killer, it is to prove Edek did not do it before the village does to him what it did to his father.

He is the campaign's most sympathetic figure and he genuinely did something (the panic, the mark). He is innocent of murder. He is also Hania's brother, which loads the endgame (see Tie to the Endgame).

## The Three False Suspects

## The Reflexive Twist

Junior is doing exactly what the committee is doing: prying at the village's buried crime from the outside. If his digging helped break Janina, the players' digging is implicated too. The case quietly asks whether investigation is itself a kind of violence. For a game about excavating buried crimes, that is the sharpest cut: to solve it is to be complicit in it.

## The Death Scene (what is on the body)

Keep the existing [Ciotka Found Dead](../events/ciotka-found-dead.md) scene, with these truths behind it:

- **Cause of death:** Luminal overdose, her own prescription. Suicide.
- **One antemortem mark** from Edek's panic (a bruise, a grip, a fall). This is the Edek strand and the thing that makes the village cry murder.
- **The door she always locked is open** because Edek fled through it.
- **Footprints to the treeline, cold mattress:** Edek's flight.
- **No death-night visitor tell.** Junior's pressure is prior; Rezeń's involvement is later. Nobody sat with her that night.
- **The cigarette butts** by the door are Junior's, from his visit the day before. But he and Rezeń both smoke Carmen, so the butts implicate the two of them equally and place neither at the death itself (they are a day old). The shared brand is the whole point: one prop, two false suspects.
- **A third Carmen** turns up later in Edek's corner ([edek-has-carmen-cigarette](../clues/clues.md#edek-has-carmen-cigarette)), which briefly seems to tie the boy to the door too. It is a false thread: Rezeń gave it to him ([rezen-gave-edek-cigarette](../clues/clues.md#rezen-gave-edek-cigarette)), a token of the butcher's disturbing fondness for the boy who fears him. It clears nothing and muddies everything, exactly as intended.

## The Cigarette Evidence: a Lens and a Trap

The smoking details are a self-contained sub-case that does real work in the main one.

**The lens (whose a butt is).** The brand marks the man. Three men smoke the premium [Carmen](../items/carmen-cigarette-butts.md): [Junior](../characters/junior.md) ([junior-smokes-carmen](../clues/clues.md#junior-smokes-carmen)), [Rezeń](../characters/butcher.md) ([butcher-smokes-carmen](../clues/clues.md#butcher-smokes-carmen)), and the [priest](../characters/priest.md), who hides the habit ([priest-smokes-carmen](../clues/clues.md#priest-smokes-carmen)). [Tadek](../characters/wujas.md) smokes the cheapest Sport ([tadek-smokes-cheapest](../clues/clues.md#tadek-smokes-cheapest)). The village will name only the first two, so a Carmen stub looks like one of the pair when it could be the priest, who was Janina's confessor and came to her often. Once players hold that, a butt anywhere on the map places a person. Reuse it deliberately. The well is littered with Carmen because Rezeń haunts it, searchable any time; Sport butts where Tadek should not be mark Tadek.

**The trap (why it convicts the innocent).** The Carmen butts at Janina's door are the most seductive physical evidence in the case, and they point at the wrong men. They tempt a verdict against Junior or Rezeń, and paired with the boy's own gifted Carmen they can even be spun against Edek. A committee that grabs the easy Carmen story hangs an innocent, which is exactly the 1954 mistake this case re-runs.

**The exculpation (how it clears the night).** The butts are weathered, dropped a day or more before she died: Junior's, from his day-before visit, not the death night. The boy's Carmen was a gift, not a habit. Weighed honestly, the same evidence that looked like proof clears all three of the death itself. The Carmen is a test of whether the committee reads evidence or reaches for a culprit.

## The Payoff: The Verdict

Fold this into the committee's report/accusation climax. The players commit a verdict, and every wrong answer has a consequence. The Carmen butts are the bait for the two middle answers: easy, physical, and false.

- **Edek** (the scapegoat): they join the mob and complete the 1954 re-run. Advances the punishment/lynch ending.
- **Rezeń** (the body-thief): they hang a guilty man for a murder he did not commit. Right about what he is, wrong about this, and the Carmen butts make it the tempting mistake.
- **Junior** (the outsider): they blame the mirror and never see themselves in it. His Carmen at the door is what frames him.
- **Suicide, driven by the buried crime** (the truth): reachable by assembling all three strands and refusing every easy answer. It re-lights 1954: she died because of what these people did and what everyone refused to say.

Unlike a normal whodunit, the reward is not catching a clever killer. It is the harder, adult recognition that no one killed her and everyone did.

## Tie to the Endgame

Edek is hunted as the killer, and he is Hania's brother. The climactic evacuation call to the operator ([Hania](../characters/jagna.md)) becomes her deciding her brother's fate, while the players may be the only ones who know he is innocent. The manhunt for Edek and the case verdict both feed the Hania reveal and the endings.
