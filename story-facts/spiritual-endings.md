# The Spiritual Endings — Remember, Grace, Forget

**Type:** Core story mechanics — endgame

Three answers to the same question: what does the valley do with its blood-guilt before the water takes it? The 1947 massacre and the 1954 lynch left the dead unnamed in the well and the guilty walking free. These three endings are the *spiritual* reckonings — separate from the worldly ones (Engineering, Justice, the Report). They are **mutually exclusive**: the valley has one soul, and it can be answered only once.

They resolve at the **flood masses / the well rite**, late game, as the water rises.

---

## The Three Answers

| Ending | What it is | Who it saves | Who it damns |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Remember** (the [Ritual](../events/the-ritual.md)) | The Lemko rite at the well — name the dead, say what was done | Justice for the **dead**: named, mourned, at rest | The guilty drown **unforgiven** |
| **Grace** (the odpust) | Day-7 general absolution at Mass — mercy for the living guilty | The **living guilty**: absolved, all of them | The dead stay unnamed; if stirred, they turn **vengeful** |
| **Forget** | Helena's cover-up holds — the truth never lands | No one is disturbed; the village dies quiet | Everyone: the dead unmourned, the guilty unrepentant, the second erasure completed |

**Each salvation is bought with the other's damnation.** You cannot give the dead their reckoning *and* the killers their mercy. Mercy for the living costs the dead their peace; justice for the dead costs the living their souls. That is the choice.

## Grace — the odpust

### The theology (fact-checked)

The word the villagers use is **odpust**. Under the hood the instrument is **general absolution** (*absolucja generalna / rozgrzeszenie ogólne*) — the priest forgiving the guilt of a whole congregation at once, without individual confession.

This is canonically valid **only in danger of death** — the battlefield chaplain, the sinking ship. **A valley about to drown is exactly that trigger.** (Note: a true *odpust zupełny* / plenary indulgence only remits temporal punishment for sins *already* forgiven and requires a state of grace — it cannot forgive the lynch. It is the wrong instrument; general absolution is the right one. The villagers still call the Day-7 rite "the odpust," and the parish-feast resonance — the whole valley crowding into the church one last time — is intentional.)

### The engine: the priest's crisis of faith

Grace fires only if **ks. Władysław Pająk** is willing to grant it, and his willingness turns on a crisis inside him: **judgment vs. mercy.**

He alone reads the flood as **literal divine judgment** — God drowning a valley built over a mass grave (Noah, the Deluge, Sodom). This is why he takes the water so seriously: every soul still in mortal sin when it comes drowns *damned*. He half-believes the valley **deserves** it — which is why his faith is collapsing.

- **Judgment wins** → the valley earned the water; he grants nothing; the guilty drown damned.
- **Mercy wins** → *everyone is redeemable*; his last task is to drag as many souls into grace as he can before the flood; he reaches for the loophole and grants the odpust.

**What tips him toward mercy: watching the guilty confess.** The men who did the worst thing in the valley kneeling before him is the proof that redemption is real. Enough of that, and he believes people are redeemable — and *everyone* means everyone, so the restored priest absolves the whole church, **Rezeń included.** (The point of "everyone is redeemable" is that it has no exceptions.)

### The mechanic: "Faith in Redemption" score

Tracked like [Dudka's Lynch Targets](../characters/neighbour.md#lynch-targets). GM tracks one score. If it clears the threshold by the **Day-7 Mass**, the priest grants the odpust.

**Starting score: 0** (faith in crisis). **Threshold: +8.** *(Starting values — tune in play.)*

| Act | Change |
|---|---|
| Tadek Gajda confesses (the penitent — easiest) | +4 |
| Emil Rzepka confesses (the forgery, his 13 years of guilt) | +2 |
| Zbigniew Gajda confesses (hard — guiltless hubris) | +4 |
| Helena Rzepka confesses (brutal — the architect, contrite but silent) | +5 |
| Stanisław Rezeń confesses (near-impossible — the void) | +5 |
| Players talk the priest through his crisis / back his faith | +2 |
| Players broker or escort a confessor to him | +1 each |
| Players honour Ciotka's funeral | +1 |
| Players care for the dying / cure Pawełek / shield Edek | +1 each |
| Players push the priest to break the seal / weaponise confession | −2 |
| A guilty one dies unconfessed (e.g. Tadek's suicide) | −2 |
| Players expose the guilty publicly with no mercy (feeds judgment) | −2 |
| Each step of the Lemko rite stirred (hag woken, dead named) | −3 |
| **Remember actually fires** | **hard block — no odpust at any score** |

No single confessor is required — the threshold is elastic and reachable many ways (two hard confessions; or one plus village mercy; etc.). Progress toward Remember drags the score down; completing Remember forecloses Grace outright.

### Note on the confessors

- **Helena** is contrite — she knows exactly what she did and that it was sin — but she swallows it in silence to protect her children, Emil, and the village order. Her confession is achievable but brutal: she must be shown there is nothing left to protect (the flood dissolves the order she damned herself for). *(Her character file still reads self-justifying — reconcile later.)*
- **Rezeń** feels no guilt, so within a strict reading he cannot make a valid confession — but the whole emotional point of a restored priest is that mercy reaches even him at the Day-7 odpust. Do not carve him out mechanically.
- **Confessing to the players is not absolution.** Only the priest, in the church, releases.

## Exclusivity — the rival officiant's plea

The valley has one collective conscience. Whichever reckoning the players commit to, **the officiant of the other ending shows up at the ceremony and pleads with them to stop** — face to face, the last human appeal before the thing becomes irreversible. No sabotage, no curse: an argument, and the players choosing to proceed anyway with their eyes open to the cost.

- **Commit to the odpust (Grace) → Babcia (Stefania Kopacz) comes.** The old Lemko woman stands up in the Catholic church and begs them not to wash the killers clean while her people lie unnamed in the well. **Proceed past her plea → the dead turn vengeful** (she will not give rest to dead wronged this way). This vengeance triggers **only if the Lemko rite was stirred** — a village that never woke the dead gets clean Grace.
- **Commit to the rite (Remember) → ks. Pająk comes.** He walks to the ruins and begs them off the old rite — for their souls, for the flood-judgment, for mercy. **Proceed past his plea → he will not grant the odpust.** Grace is foreclosed; the guilty drown unforgiven.

Whichever ceremony you complete, you do it **over the tears of the person who speaks for the other ending.** That is the tragedy made personal.
