# The Committee — Player Characters

**Type:** Core story fact — player entry point

## Formation

After prof. Tadeusz Bieńkowski's discovery that the flood zone **may** swallow %NEW_VILLAGE%, the state scrambles a response. A committee is formed and dispatched to the village to prepare for the worst case. **por. Witold Skowron** (SB) is assigned as their handler — officially to "facilitate cooperation," in reality to control what they see and what they report.

## Mandate

The committee has three tasks, and the first two are kept in **two separate, mandatory ledgers** — every household must appear in both before it can be closed:

1. **The People Ledger (census)** — Who lives in each household and how they are related. Drives **evacuation and rehousing**: the state moves *people*, so it must know who physically sleeps under each roof, and who belongs to whom.
2. **The Property Ledger (title / damage)** — Who is the rightful owner of each parcel, and **where is that owner** if they are not the occupant. Drives **compensation**: money follows title. An occupant with no title gets nothing; an abandoned parcel reverts to the state.
3. **Evacuation planning** — Prepare a relocation plan in case the flooding is confirmed.

A fourth, underlying task belongs to **one of the players — a geologist**. They must take field measurements (elevation, soil, terrain) around %NEW_VILLAGE% and %OLD_VILLAGE% to determine whether the flood will actually reach the village. This survey at the village outskirts is what will confirm or deny the need for evacuation — and it requires going **into the field**, near the ruins, near the well.

## What the Village Knows

**The villagers have no idea they are about to be flooded.** They know about the Solina Dam — everyone does — but they believe only %OLD_VILLAGE% and the lowlands are in the flood zone. %NEW_VILLAGE% was never part of the original displacement plan. The committee's arrival is the first sign that something has changed.

por. Witold Skowron instructs the committee to **be delicate** — don't lead with the evacuation. Start with the census, the damage assessment. Let the villagers think this is routine paperwork. Break the news gradually, once trust is established.

This means the players walk into a village that doesn't yet know its world is ending — and they're carrying the death sentence in their briefcases.

## The Two Ledgers — and the Gap Between Them

The committee's paperwork isn't bureaucratic busywork — it's **the document that will determine the village's fate**, and it is the game's third ending register: the **bureaucratic reckoning**, separate from the flood and the guilt. Where the rite gives the dead *rest* and the odpust gives the living *grace*, the ledgers force the **state** to admit — on paper it cannot later erase — who existed and who owned what.

The two ledgers go back to the state and decide:

- **Who gets compensated** and how much (Property ledger).
- **Who gets relocated** and where (People ledger).
- **Who officially exists** — anyone in neither ledger doesn't exist to the state. No entry, no compensation, no new home.

**The discovery engine is the gap between the two ledgers.** Both are mandatory per household, and an honest committee cannot close a household until the two entries agree. In a normal village they always do — occupant *is* owner, done. In **this** village they can't:

- **People ledger:** a living occupant sleeps in the house.
- **Property ledger:** the rightful owner was "resettled 1947" or "departed" — but is actually **in the well.**

Lay the two side by side and the contradiction is structural, not accusatory. A living occupant on a dead man's "resettled" plot. A child on the People ledger who is officially no one's. An owner "departed" by a forged declaration who never left the valley. **The committee doesn't accuse anyone — it just fills the form honestly, and the form refuses to close.**

This is plausible to the state precisely because the false answers **hide inside a true pattern**: across post-displacement Bieszczady, "resettled" is the ordinary, accepted disposition for abandoned parcels. The committee asking *who owned this, where did they go* is routine title work. The committee **discovering that the accepted answer is a lie** is the crime surfacing — quietly, through arithmetic, not through an accusation anyone can suppress.

Two crime-surfaces, one per ledger:

| Ledger | Question it forces | Lies it surfaces |
|---|---|---|
| **People** | Who lives here, and how are they related? | [Edek's](../characters/glupek.md) parentage (recording him → the lynch); [Janina's](../characters/ciotka.md) single-woman-with-the-best-plot; [Barbara](../characters/barbara.md) refusing to name Pawełek's father |
| **Property** | Who owns this, and where is the owner? | The dead in the well: occupant ≠ owner; 1947 Lemko owners "resettled"; [Edward Barnaś](../characters/soldier.md) "departed" via forged declaration |

### The Flood Line — the scope that swallows everything

The state does not want the whole valley surveyed. The instruction is narrow: **document only property below the flood line.** On paper the line is drawn low — %OLD_VILLAGE% and the lowlands, the parcels the state already conceded in the original displacement plan. Everything uphill, all of %NEW_VILLAGE%, is officially *above* the water and out of scope. Skowron likes this rule: it is a fence around the paperwork, and it conveniently keeps the committee's pens away from most of the living village.

**The geologist breaks the fence.** The field survey — the fourth task — reveals the real flood line sits far higher than the official one: the water is coming for the **entire village.** Every house, every plot, every parcel is below the line after all.

And the scoping rule, once the line is redrawn, **inverts.** "Document only below the flood line" was meant to limit the survey; with the true line established, it now *mandates* total coverage. Skowron's own fence becomes the warrant that opens every door — he can no longer wave the committee off any parcel, because the rule he set says everything below the line must be recorded, and everything is below the line. The instrument of containment becomes the instrument of exposure.

Two horrors land in the same stroke: the village learns it is **wholly doomed**, and the committee gains the mandate to document **every** contested title — including the ones that lead to the well.

Writing the dead in is not the players bending the form — it's the form's own required fields. **[Wanda Mazur](../characters/secondary-characters.md)** asking to count her dead husband and **[Emil](../characters/painter.md)** wanting Hania written down are villagers pointing at entries the ledgers legitimately want.

This gives the players enormous power they may not immediately realize. The pen in their hand is life or death for these families — and it is also the one record the state cannot un-write once it is filed and sent. And it gives the villagers an enormous reason to cooperate — or to manipulate. Everyone wants to be in the ledger. Everyone wants their property valued generously. And some people do **not** want field 3 answered at all.

## What This Means in Practice

The committee has a legitimate reason to:
- **Knock on every door.** Census requires face-to-face contact with every household.
- **Ask personal questions.** Family composition, who lives where, how long they've been here.
- **Inspect every building and plot.** Including abandoned structures, outbuildings, and the ruins of %OLD_VILLAGE%.
- **Talk to people who don't want to talk.** The evacuation is mandatory — cooperation isn't optional.

This gives the players a bureaucratic skeleton key to the entire village. They're not detectives — they're clerks with clipboards. But the clipboard opens every door.

## por. Witold Skowron's Role

por. Witold Skowron manages the committee. He decides their schedule, arranges housing, provides introductions to Zbigniew Gajda and the village. On paper, he's helpful. In practice, he's steering them away from anything that might surface the 1947 massacre — the state secret he's there to protect.

His control is **lopsided across the two ledgers.** The **People ledger** he lets run freely — evacuation is his cover story, and counting the living costs him nothing. The **Property ledger** is where he pushes: keep field 3 reading "resettled," steer the survey away from the %OLD_VILLAGE% parcels and the ruins, discourage the geologist from lingering near the well. Title is the field that leads to the dead, so title is the field he polices.

His sharpest tool is the **flood line** — the rule that only property below it gets documented, drawn low to keep scope small. But that tool turns in his hand: once the geologist proves the whole village sits below the true line, the same rule forces total coverage, and Skowron loses his grounds to wave the committee off any parcel (see [the flood line](#the-flood-line--the-scope-that-swallows-everything)). After that his obstruction has to get personal — schedules, pressure, veiled threats — because the paperwork itself no longer gives him cover.

He doesn't know about the lynch. That's the villagers' secret, not his — but the Property ledger is where the two cover-ups collide, because the 1954 owner "departed" and the 1947 owners "resettled" sit on the same page.

## The Tension

Two cover-ups, one committee:

| Secret | Who's hiding it | Who's steering the committee away |
|---|---|---|
| The massacre (1947) | The state | **por. Witold Skowron** — actively |
| The lynch (1954) | The villagers | **Zbigniew Gajda** — desperately |

The players are caught between two forces that both want them to do their job quickly, ask no questions, and leave. The question is whether the cracks show before the water rises.
